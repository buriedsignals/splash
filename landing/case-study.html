The situation

The situation, in a heading of its own.

The opening paragraph carries more weight than the ones after it, so it takes the `lead` class and a size up. Use it for what the desk was facing: the material it had, the slot it had to fill, and the thing that was missing.

The paragraphs that follow are ordinary body copy at the reading measure. Write as many as the case needs; the block grows with them and nothing has to be re-measured. Two or three is usually the whole of it, and a fourth is generally a second block wanting to be written.

Anything a normal editor produces will come out in the house style here — headings, lists, links, pictures — so this can be pasted in rather than marked up by hand.

How the two days went

How it went, in three or four beats.

A beat gets a heading and a paragraph

Use `h3` for each step of the session and keep the heading short enough to read as a label. The paragraph under it is where the detail goes: what was asked, what came back, and what changed because of it. Beats are prose and not a table — a table of decisions is a thing to audit, and nobody reads a case study to audit it.

One of them should be a refusal

A case that only shows what worked proves nothing; anything produces something. Whatever the agent declined to draw, and the reason it gave, belongs here in the same voice as the rest.

The last one is the delivery

What came back at the end, in what forms, and what happens the next time the same file arrives.

What was typed

Five sentences. That was the session.

Every sentence the journalist actually typed, in order, and in one line each what came back. Copied, not reconstructed — this is the section that keeps the page a how-to rather than a story.

  1. The first thing you typed, word for word. One line on what came back.
  2. The second, including anything you asked it to work within. One line on what came back.
  3. An instruction it refused, if there was one. The reason it gave, and what it offered instead.
  4. The choice you made, in your own words. One line on what came back.
  5. The approval, and what you asked it to hand over. One line on what came back.
