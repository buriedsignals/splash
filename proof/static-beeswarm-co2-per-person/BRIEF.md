# Beat — Les 6 pays au-dessus de 20 t de CO₂ par personne pèsent 0,6 % de l'humanité

**Type:** beeswarm (chart). **Medium/format:** chart / **static**. **Size:** landscape
(1920 x 1080), pinned in the front matter above.

The first `beeswarm` beat in this tree.

## It bins nothing, and that is the pair with the histogram

`proof/static-carbon-footprint-spread` draws this same per-capita file as a **histogram**. A bin has
no identity: it can say *127 countries emit under 4 tonnes* and it can never say **which**, nor how
many people live in them. A beeswarm keeps every unit, so the **shape** and the **named case** are on
one plate — which is exactly what the reference this form is filed against does with 8 500 schools.

The population column is joined in so the second channel means something: **the circle's area is the
country's population**. That is the reading the histogram cannot reach, and the one the claim rests
on.

## The claim

**The six countries above 20 tonnes of CO₂ per person hold 0.6 % of humanity.** The largest circle
on the plate is India — 1.4 billion people at 2.1 t, *below* the median of the 213 countries (3.1 t).
And the world average, 4.6 t, already sits above what 64 % of people emit.

Every number is derived from the frozen file before a mark is drawn, and the plate refuses to render
if the six countries hold 1 % or more, if the average sits above less than 60 % of people, or if the
largest circle is not below the country median.

**Both callouts are derived, not chosen**: the biggest circle on the plate and the farthest one out.
A hand-picked callout is an editorial claim the plate cannot check; these two are the marks a
reader's eye lands on anyway, and naming them answers the question the shape raises.

## A swarm is only as honest as its packing

Three measurements hold the encoding up:

- **A mark is pushed only across the axis, never along it.** A swarm that slides a circle sideways
  to make room has lied about the one thing it measures.
- **The largest circles are laid down first.** A big circle placed late has nowhere left to go and
  ends up thrown to the edge of the band, where it reads as a value it does not have.
- **The radius is a ladder, not a taste.** Area is population, so the radius runs on a square root;
  how large the largest circle may be is decided by whether the whole packed swarm still fits its
  band. Eight rungs are walked from generous to mean and the first that fits is taken. The smallest
  countries are floored at a radius a reader can see — a distortion, and one the ladder prints rather
  than hides.

## The card sits above the field, because inside it there was never room

The first version placed each callout beside its own circle. The largest circle is **by definition
where the field is thickest**, so there was no clear seat near it and the card landed on the swarm it
was naming. A card written over the distribution it describes is not a card, it is a stain on the
evidence. Both cards now have room reserved above the band, each centred on its own circle, clamped
inside the plate, pushed apart from each other along the axis, with a hairline leader down to the
circle's edge — **a card is allowed to move, a circle is not.**

## What the reference gave, and what it did not

The `beeswarm` form's single filed reference is **ABC News' *Rich school, poor school*** — one
publication, below this base's floor of two independent ones, so the form gets **no family rules of
its own**. It contributes three measured imports, and the plate takes all three:

- **The axis name and its ticks are one size, separated by weight, both tracked** — measured on that
  plate's own elements (`ABCSans 12/700` for `INCOME`, `ABCSansRegular 12/400` for `$50m`). Sharing
  one row means they can collide, and when they do **the tick gives way**: a tick is one reading of a
  scale a reader can interpolate, the name is the only thing that says what the scale is. The ladder
  prints how many ticks the name cost.
- **State the encodings in the running prose, and spend no plate on a key.** There is no size legend
  here. The standfirst says what a circle is and what its area means, in the sentence the reader is
  already reading — the ABC's own move, in its own standfirst.
- **A callout card, not a callout label** — taken in the form a static plate can carry: the name in
  the mark's own weight, then the two numbers that make the mark worth naming. The photograph the ABC
  puts in its card is not something this base can produce, and is not faked.

What the reference does with **colour** is not taken, and `PALETTE.md` records why: the ABC's hue
carries a school sector, and this plate has no category to carry. So the field is one colour and
`the-subject-is-ringed-not-recoloured` does the rest — the reference's own rule, read back at it.

`the-distribution-is-furniture-and-the-case-is-ink` (Nature, NSIDC) — the swarm is drawn quietly and
the accent is spent on the two cases and on the world average, which is what the headline argues
about.

## Source

(2023) on the ISO code, frozen beside this beat as `data.csv`. All 213 countries in the emissions
file matched a population row; none was dropped.
