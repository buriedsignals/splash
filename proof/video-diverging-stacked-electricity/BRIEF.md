# Beat — En France, le nucléaire pèse plus que le fossile et le renouvelable réunis (video)

**Type:** diverging stacked bar (chart). **Medium/format:** chart / **video**. **Size:** landscape (1920 × 1080).

Same subject, same frozen file (`../static-diverging-stacked-electricity/data.csv`, Ember and Energy Institute via Our World
in Data) and the same assertions as `proof/static-diverging-stacked-electricity`: six 2024 electricity mixes as shares, every
source classified into fossil (gas, oil, coal), nuclear, renewables (bioenergy, other, hydropower, solar, wind); each row
sums to 100 %; the rows ordered by fossil share; the country with the largest nuclear share (France) has more nuclear than
fossil and renewables put together. One scale (pixels per percentage point) for every length, in every shot.

## The argument

A diverging stacked bar is an ordinary 100 % bar re-anchored on the level that belongs to neither side — so the video opens on
the six whole mixes laid from one left edge, slides each, keeping every length, until its nuclear sits astride the anchor
(the lean appears), then takes France's two sides off its bar and lays them end to end under its nuclear: they stop short.

## The picture — shots, not a page

1. **The title card** (from frame 0, 1.5 s).
2. **The story** — six rows, the names at the left; over them « 2024 », then the three side names over the anchor.
3. **No end card** — the video ends on the whole diverging chart, France's nuclear ringed; the credit on one line. About 19.5 s.
