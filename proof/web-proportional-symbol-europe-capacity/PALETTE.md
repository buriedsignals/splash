The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,
`ground: "#FFFFFF"`). `origin: newsroom` says who chose them. This is the palette
`composeDirections` reconciles the three filed directions against; the delivered page is drawn in
whichever direction governs it, never in this one.

**The subject carries no convention, so the newsroom's own colour is the answer.** Low-carbon
capacity has no palette a European reader already holds — no party colour, no flag, no
red-hot/blue-cold reading a temperature map could borrow. Nothing was proposed ahead of the house
accent, and that is stated here rather than left as a silence.

**ONE HUE, ONE CHROMA, AND NO RAMP — because on this type colour is not the encoding.** Forty-one
circles, one quantity, and the quantity is carried by AREA. A symbol map that also graded its fill
would be stating the same number twice, in two channels a reader would then have to reconcile; a
symbol map that graded it by fill INSTEAD would be a choropleth with the geography thrown away. So
every mark on this page is the same colour, and the only thing that differs between two marks is
how big they are. The key underwrites that: three named magnitudes, no continuous ramp.

**The accent is measured on the COMPOSITE, not on its own fill, and that is not a detail.**
`an-overlap-accumulates-rather-than-occluding` needs the symbols translucent — 0,5 here — so a
reader can see that two fleets overlap rather than that one has eaten the other. An opacity laid
over a calibrated colour is a different colour: this tree has already shipped 1,75:1 twice that
way, on a pair that measured 3:1 before the opacity was applied. Every number below is therefore
read off the composite the reader actually sees, over the basemap's own land.

| | the dose of ink the pointed mark takes | composite against the mark at rest | composite against the land |
|---|---|---|---|
| creme | 0,26 | 1,145:1 | 2,507:1 |
| rapport | 0,28 | 1,150:1 | 2,527:1 |
| nocturne | 0,40 | 1,146:1 | 3,687:1 |

The mark that answers a pointer is a dose of the direction's own ink mixed into the symbol fill,
searched at render time for the smallest dose that moves 1,14:1 off the resting composite — and
REFUSED if the moved mark ends up closer to the basemap than the resting one did, which would make
the circle a reader is pointing at the least visible thing on the plate. That search lives in the
component, in the one place that knows both the opacity and the land under it, and the page throws
rather than ships a state a reader could not see.

**Nothing else on this page is chromatic.** The land, its edges, the water and the key's own border
are steps off the direction's own ground (`mix(ground, ink, 0.1 / 0.24 / 0.03)`), which is
`the-basemap-gives-up-its-contrast` written as arithmetic: the plate is quiet enough that every
symbol reads against it. The printed names and figures are the direction's ink, lifted to the text
floor against that ground. Not one of these is a hex literal in the component — every one is
derived from the two colours in the front matter above and the direction that governs the render.
