The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,
`ground: "#FFFFFF"`). `origin: newsroom` says who chose them. This is the palette
`composeDirections` reconciles the three filed directions against; the delivered page is drawn in
whichever direction governs it, never in this one.

**The subject carries no convention, so the house colours stand.** Sex is one of the few subjects in
this catalogue where a reader might think they hold one — the pink-and-blue pair — and it is not a
convention this beat will spend. It is a stereotype rather than a reading aid, it fails as a pair for
a red-green colour-vision-deficient reader, and it would put the argument's whole weight on colour in
a type whose two groups are already told apart by POSITION: left and right of a centre line. The type
sheet says exactly that — the mirrored layout "already carries the group distinction through position
… the colour pairing is reinforcing a difference the shape already makes, not carrying it alone".

**One achromatic half and one chromatic half.** The men's side is a neutral step off the direction's
own ground; the women's side is the direction's accent, softened toward that ground. A pair with one
achromatic member is legible to every kind of colour-vision deficiency there is, which is the
checked-as-a-pair test the type sheet asks for, and it survives greyscale because the two halves
differ in lightness as well as in hue. Neither half is the accent at full strength: the accent at
full strength is the page's argument ink, and it is spent on the peak note and on the crossing the
fold names.

**And the fills are not free to be anything that clears the ground.** This page lays a solid outline
ACROSS both halves — the folded profile — so each fill has a second obligation the other beats in
this tree do not carry: the line has to stay legible ON it. Each half is therefore taken as far from
the ground as the line's own 3:1 floor against that fill allows, and never nearer than its own 3:1
floor against the ground. On `nocturne` that window is nearly shut: the accent half sat at 6.46:1 on
the ground, which left the white line 2.75:1 over it, and the search pulled it back to `#3ea49c`
(5.92:1 on the ground, 3.00:1 under the line). The component throws rather than ship a line a reader
loses the moment it enters a bar.

Nothing else on the page is chromatic. The axis labels, the centre line, the gridlines and the
ground the annotations print on are steps off the direction's own ground, computed by
`deriveFurniture` at render time and never written here as a literal.
