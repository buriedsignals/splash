The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,
`ground: "#FFFFFF"`). `origin: newsroom` says who chose them. This is the palette
`composeDirections` reconciles the three filed directions against; the delivered page is drawn in
whichever direction governs it, never in this one — so every number below is measured on what
`creme`, `nocturne` and `rapport` actually paint, not on the pair in this frontmatter.

**No convention is taken, and that is a decision.** `palette`'s subject-fit branch fires on
"electricity" and "renewables" and is refused for the fill, for the reason the static brother
settled: the quantity here is ONE share on ONE ramp, so the ramp is built between the direction's
own ground and its own accent and nothing else on the plate is chromatic. Unlike the static
brother, this page has no sea and no land — a tile cartogram's geometry IS the data — so the one
grounded convention that beat does take (`water-is-a-tint-not-a-grey`) has nothing here to colour.

**Five steps, and five is the ceiling.** `mix(ground, accent, 0.14 + i/4 × 0.86)`, measured on the
delivered pages:

| | `creme` (ground `#fffcee`) | `nocturne` (ground `#111044`) | `rapport` (ground `#ffffff`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| under 30 % | `#dfe5e6` · 1,24:1 | `#1a2d55` · 1,31:1 | `#e0e8ef` · 1,24:1 |
| 30–50 % | `#adc1da` · 1,79:1 | `#275a70` · 2,36:1 | `#afc5d6` · 1,78:1 |
| 50–70 % | `#7b9ece` · 2,68:1 | `#34878b` · 4,22:1 | `#7fa2bd` · 2,69:1 |
| 70–94 % | `#497ac2` · 4,21:1 | `#42b3a5` · 6,96:1 | `#4f7fa4` · 4,28:1 |
| 94 % and over | `#1757b6` · 6,64:1 | `#4fe0c0` · 10,80:1 | `#1f5c8b` · 7,09:1 |

Step to step the separation is 1,44:1 to 1,79:1 — far off the 1,007:1 a seven-step ramp measured
elsewhere in this base, and the reason there are five steps and not seven. At five, the key stops
nuancing and NAMES each band in the data's own units, which is the treatment
`the-key-prints-its-breaks-in-the-data-s-units` and the decoder line this variant's type sheet asks
for in the map's own furniture.

**The lightest class does not carry itself, and the edge is what makes it a cell.** 1,24:1 against
the ground is not a visible fill; a reader sees that square because it is STROKED. The edge is
`#adaba2` / `#5d5c80` / `#adadad` — 2,24:1, 2,80:1 and 2,24:1 against the ground, and 1,81:1,
2,13:1 and 1,81:1 against the lightest fill it encloses. This is the owner's sixth ruling taken at
its word: no ink passed the floor as a fill, so what does the work is a stroke, not another ink.

**The hollow cell is drawn outside the ramp, and so is its answer.** Ukraine has no 2024 reading:
it is `fill: none` with the same edge dashed `3 3`, never the lowest band. A square with no ink
cannot darken, so under a pointer it lifts on its EDGE instead — 1,180:1 on `creme`, 1,209:1 on
`nocturne`, 1,223:1 on `rapport`, all sought to the same floor as the filled cells.

**Every hover lift is sought, never fixed.** A dose is walked up in 0,02 steps until it clears
1,18:1 off the cell's OWN painted fill, and thrown if no dose does. Measured across the 40 filled
cells: 1,183:1 to 1,232:1 on `creme`, 1,183:1 to 1,275:1 on `nocturne`, 1,183:1 to 1,218:1 on
`rapport`. On `nocturne` the dose runs toward the GROUND rather than the ink, because the brightest
teal has nowhere darker to go that a reader would read as the same cell.

**A name is an ink chosen per cell, against the cell.** The country name and its value are placed
on top of five different fills, so the ink is picked per square and measured against that square,
never against the page. Worst case over the three directions: 4,90:1 (`rapport`, Spain), 4,85:1
(`creme`, Spain), 4,51:1 (`nocturne`, Germany) — all at 12px/600, all over the 4,5:1 floor for
body text, and the worst of the three is the dark page rather than the light ones.

Everything else — the title, the caveat, the pills, the average line, the revealed sentence — is a
step off the direction's own ground, computed by `deriveFurniture` at render time and never written
here as a literal.
