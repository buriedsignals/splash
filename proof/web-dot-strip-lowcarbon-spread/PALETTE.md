The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,
`ground: "#FFFFFF"`). `origin: newsroom` says who chose them. This is the palette
`composeDirections` reconciles the three filed directions against; the delivered page is drawn in
whichever direction governs it, never in this one.

## One hue, and this type spends it on cases rather than on categories

`chart-beat/references/types/dot-strip.md` settles the category question for this type before it is
asked: *"All dots share a single colour — this type doesn't spend colour on category separation,
since the lanes themselves already do that job through position."* This page draws two lanes and
thirty-two dots and nothing on it is coloured by what it is. The channel is spent on **two cases and
nowhere else**, and the field is left achromatic:

- **the field** — fourteen countries on each rail, a step off the direction's own ground, raised
  until what the page PAINTS clears the non-text floor (below). Not a hue anyone chose, on purpose:
  `the-distribution-is-furniture-and-the-case-is-ink`.
- **the two cases** — **Poland**, the subject, which is the 2000 floor the headline is about; and
  **France**, derived in the runner as the country that falls furthest down the control's ladder
  (82,4 points, against 5,7 for the smallest, Ireland) and asserted there before it is drawn. Both
  are in the direction's own accent and both are drawn in **every rung of the control**, which
  `web-discipline.md` requires of an accent on the subject and which is also the whole point: under
  « le bas-carbone » Poland is the floor of the 2024 rail and France sits near the top; under « ni la
  bioénergie » they have swapped sides.

The static sibling's own refusal carries over unchanged: sixteen entities would need sixteen hues,
METHOD correction 28 forbids inventing a palette this base does not own, and
`context-in-neutral-at-the-subject-scale` is what survives.

## The opacity is real here, so every number below is the composite

This is the one type in the catalogue whose sheet asks for transparency by name — *"enough
transparency that overlapping points still show through each other … overlap here is implied by
transparency, not resolved by pushing points apart"* — which is exactly what separates it from the
beeswarm sibling, whose packer guarantees no two marks touch and whose `PALETTE.md` therefore records
removing an opacity that was buying nothing.

Here it buys the type's own honesty, and it costs the thing this branch has already paid for twice
(1,75:1 and 2,19:1 on two beats whose accent was calibrated and then faded): **the colour a reader
receives is not the fill, it is the fill composited onto the ground.** So the fill is searched until
the COMPOSITE clears the floor, and **every colour in the table below is the composite** — what the
page paints, at `fill-opacity: 0.72`, measured off the emitted HTML rather than off the derivation.

## The case floor is this page's own number, and it was found by measuring rather than by looking

An accent normally has one floor to clear: the ground. On this type it has two, and the second is
the one that matters — **a dot strip's accent is not marking a bar against a background, it is
marking two dots among thirty-two identical ones.** Painted at 0,72 and calibrated against the ground
alone, the accent measured **1,18:1 against its own field on `creme` and 1,25:1 on `rapport`**: the
opacity eating precisely the separation the accent was calibrated for, which is this branch's
recorded defect in a third costume.

So the component searches the first adjustment of the direction's accent that clears **both** 3:1
against the ground **and a declared 1,6:1 against the painted field**, and refuses if none does.
There is no WCAG floor for one mark against another, so 1,6 is the beat's own number and is written
down as such. What it costs is visible: on the two light directions the accent comes out deeper than
the direction files it (`#1757B6` → `#0E3671`, `#1F5C8B` → `#143D5C`) and the contrast against the
ground rises to 5,15:1 and 4,98:1. On `nocturne` the floor is not binding — the mint already stands
1,96:1 off its field — so the direction's own accent stands untouched.

## What answers the pointer, and the dose is searched rather than typed

The dot itself darkens, off **its own painted colour**, and no ring is plastered over it: the owner
refused that three times, and refused a fixed dose that measured 1,104:1 on `nocturne`. There are two
fills, so there are two generated rules, and neither is an inline style, which would beat every
selector. The search takes the smallest step of the direction's ink whose composite clears 1,14:1
against the composite it came from — so what is measured is what a reader sees, not what the
stylesheet says. On `nocturne` it runs the other way, toward a lighter mint, and lands at the floor,
which is the floor doing its job rather than a number anybody chose.

## Nothing else on the page is chromatic

The gridlines, the two rails, the six statistic ticks, the span bars, the control's pills and its
revealed sentences are steps off the direction's own ground, computed by `deriveFurniture` at render
time and never written here as a literal. The span bars in particular are furniture rather than
accent on purpose: the bar is the reading this control hands back, and a reading drawn in the colour
reserved for the subject would say that the span belongs to Poland.
