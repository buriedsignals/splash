# Centring — the verdict, after opening all eight

`CENTRING-MEASUREMENTS.md` is generated by `centring-probe.mjs`. This file is not: it is what a
person saw when the PNGs were opened, plus the one thing that turned out to decide the question and
is not visible in any of them.

The finding this acts on, from `PORTRAIT-VERDICT.md`: the B0 arm — the plot clamped into the aspect
its type supports, no editorial furniture added — brings the chart's shape back but reads like "a
graphic that failed to load". Opening it says why: the content is flush to the TOP and roughly 45%
of the frame below it is blank. The proposal under test is that it is not the quantity of void that
fails, it is that the void is on ONE SIDE — centred, the same void becomes a margin.

Open, side by side, in this order:

- `h-b0-capped-bare.png` · `h-v1-block-centred.png` · `h-v2-plot-centred.png` · `h-v3-safe-band-centred.png`
- `l-b0-capped-bare.png` · `l-v1-block-centred.png` · `l-v2-plot-centred.png` · `l-v3-safe-band-centred.png`

The ranking is not here: `PORTRAIT-VERDICT.md` already decided that its answer is the transposed
FORM, and a transposed ranking fills the frame, so it has no void to place.

---

## The short answer

**Centring wins, V1 over V2, and the same way on BOTH types — there is no per-type split between
the two variants asked for.** And then the functional constraint moves the answer again:

| | verdict |
|---|---|
| histogram | **V1 beats B0. V2 loses to both.** And V3 beats V1 once the platform is in the picture |
| line | **V1 beats B0. V2 loses to both.** Same, and V3 fits the platform's band exactly where V1 does not |

> **The centre of the frame is not the centre of the post.** Meta publishes a safe zone that
> reserves the top 14% and the **bottom 35%** of a 9:16 frame for its own interface. Centred on the
> page (V1), the chart's x axis lands inside that bottom reserve. Centred on the BAND instead
> (`v3`), everything a reader needs clears the chrome on both platforms. **The right composition
> here is decided by the platform, not by the page** — and the page's own centre is 8-10 points of
> frame height too low.

discussed separately below.

**The clamp is not what is being tested and it did not move.** The plot's aspect is 1.1:1 in all
four histogram arms and 0.8:1 in all four line arms, read off the emitted files rather than assumed.
The variants are made by baking a vertical offset into a subset of B0's own coordinates — nothing is
re-laid-out, no component is edited — and there are three separate checks that this is
composition-only, because the whole comparison is worthless if it is not:

- The arithmetic the table performs on itself: the histogram's plot centre moves 31.1% → 52.4%,
  which is 409.0px on a 1920 frame, which is the shift to the pixel. Same for the line, 28.7% →
  50.9% against a 426.3px shift.
- `assertCompositionOnly` in the runner blanks every vertical coordinate in B0 and in the variant
  and requires the two to be **byte-identical** afterwards — same colours, same font sizes, same
  widths, same wrap, same document order — while requiring them to DIFFER before blanking, so a
  recomposer that did nothing could not pass.
- That check was verified by breaking it: making the shift touch `x` as well as `y` turns it red on
  the first arm with the offending line quoted. A check that never goes red proves nothing.

**And one number that was not expected.** V1 and V2 put the PLOT in almost exactly the same place —
52.4% against 52.9% on the histogram, 50.9% against 50.1% on the line. Once the plot is clamped, the
header is small enough relative to the leftover that centring the whole block and centring the plot
alone land within a percent of each other. **So the only real difference between the two variants is
where the TITLE sits**, and the question "V1 or V2" is exactly the question "top or centre for the
headline" and nothing else.

## What I saw

### The histogram

**B0.** The bars start under a header pinned at the top, the shape reads correctly, and then the
frame just stops: from the x-axis title down, 874px — 45% of the frame — is white. Nothing about it
says a decision was made. It reads as a graphic whose bottom half failed to arrive.

**V1 (`h-v1-block-centred`).** The same drawing with 465px of air above and 465px below. It stops
looking broken. Whether it looks GOOD on a blank page is a fair question and my honest answer is
"acceptable, not handsome" — a 24% top margin on a page is a lot of nothing, and the block reads as
a card floating in a tall frame rather than as a composed poster. But the failure mode is gone: the
void is now symmetrical, and a symmetrical void reads as a margin. The owner's reading holds.

**V2 (`h-v2-plot-centred`).** Worse than both, and it introduces a defect neither of the others has.
The title, standfirst and source sit at the top exactly as in B0; then there is a 450px hole; then
the chart. The header is orphaned — it reads as a caption that lost its picture, and the eye has to
jump a third of the frame to connect the sentence to the bars. B0 at least kept the header attached
to the chart it describes. This is the clearest single result in the probe: **V2 spends the void in
the one place it must not go, between the words and the thing they are about.**

**V3 (`h-v3-safe-band-centred`).** Built after the safe-area numbers came back. Header at 13.7%,
chart below it, 35% of the frame left clear at the bottom. On the page it looks top-weighted next to
V1 — the void is unequal again, 263 above against 666 below. But it is unequal *on purpose and in
the right direction*, and the 666px it leaves at the bottom is within 6px of the 672px the platform
takes for its caption and buttons. Held next to V1 with the platform's chrome in mind, this is the
one I would publish.

### The line

**B0 (`l-b0-capped-bare`, rendered here for the first time — the earlier probe gave the line a
stretch arm and a furnished arm and no bare control).** Same failure, slightly worse: 909px, 47% of
the frame, empty between the x-axis labels and the source credit at the bottom margin. The credit
sitting alone down there makes it worse rather than better — it proves the frame did not end, it was
just not used.

**V1 (`l-v1-block-centred`).** Clearly the best of the first three. The descent occupies the middle
of the frame, the end label and its dot travel with the path correctly, and the source credit stays
where a credit belongs, at the bottom margin. Note the honest wrinkle in the "equal void" claim for
this type: the block has 483px above and 483px below, but the lower band is not empty — the credit
is in it. On the page that reads fine; the credit is quiet enough to be a footer rather than a
second element.

**V2 (`l-v2-plot-centred`).** Fails the same way as the histogram's V2 and for the same reason. One
short title alone at the top, then 400px of nothing, then the chart. Of all seven renders this is
the one that most looks like a mistake.

**V3 (`l-v3-safe-band-centred`).** Same shape of improvement as the histogram's, and for this type
it is not a compromise at all — the line's whole block is 954.5px tall and the band is 979px, so it
**fits**, with room to spare.

## The safe-area finding, and its standing

Two rows, and they do not have the same standing. That difference is part of the result.

| platform | top | bottom | safe band | standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram Stories + Reels | 269px | 672px | 269–1248px — **51%** of the frame | **published by the platform**, as 14% / 35% / 6% per side; the pixels are that percentage on 1080×1920 |
| TikTok | 130px | 484px | 130–1436px — 68% of the frame | **not published as pixels** — see below |

- Meta publishes one unified safe zone covering Stories and Reels and states it in PERCENTAGES:
  14% top, 35% bottom, 6% each side. On a 1080×1920 canvas that is 269px / 672px / 65px.
  <https://www.facebook.com/business/help/980593475366490/>
- TikTok does **not** publish pixel figures. Its own in-feed specification says the safe zone "is
  determined by video dimension type, ad caption length, and interactive add-on usage. The longer
  the caption, the smaller the safe zone will be", and directs creators to its preview tool and
  downloadable templates instead. The 130 / 484 numbers above are the figures third-party guides
  converge on, carried here as an INDICATION, not a specification.
  <https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-reservation-in-feed-ads-reach-frequency>
  Note the direction of the uncertainty: TikTok says a longer caption makes the zone SMALLER, so the
  real bottom reserve is that or worse, never better.
- **Meta's band sits inside TikTok's cited one**, so a composition that satisfies Meta satisfies both.
  That is why V3 is centred on Meta's and only Meta's.
- One caution against over-reading these numbers: Meta's safe zone is a **text-free** recommendation
  for ads — content outside it is not clipped or hidden, it is at RISK of being covered by the
  profile row at the top and the caption, buttons and progress bar at the bottom. "Under the chrome"
  in the generated table means at risk, not deleted.

**What that does to each arm**, from the per-platform table:

- **B0 and V2 put the HEADLINE under the top chrome on both platforms.** Their title's top edge is
  at 2.9% of the frame — 56px, well inside Meta's 269px reserve and inside TikTok's 130px. The most
  important element in the graphic is the one the interface sits on. This alone disqualifies V2:
  it keeps the title exactly where B0 had it, so it inherits B0's worst functional defect while
  adding a new compositional one.
- **V1 rescues the title on both platforms** (top edge 24-25%, clear of both reserves) **and pays
  for it with the x axis.** On Meta's band the histogram's tick labels and axis title, and the
  line's year labels and end label, fall into the bottom reserve. That is a real trade and V1 takes
  the right side of it — a headline is worth more than an axis title — but it is a trade.
- **V3 pays for nothing.** On TikTok's cited band the histogram's V3 has NOTHING under the chrome at
  all, and the line's V3 has only its bottom-pinned source credit. On Meta's published band, the
  line's V3 is entirely clear except that same credit.

**And here is the one place the two types genuinely differ.** The line's block is 954.5px of ink
against Meta's 979px band, so it fits. The histogram's is 990.4px and **overflows by 11.4px**, split
evenly by the centring, which is why `h-v3`'s title top edge lands at 263px against the 269px line —
it misses by 6px. So for the histogram the safe band is not merely a placement rule, it is a SIZE
budget the current clamp very slightly blows. Twelve pixels is nothing to fix (a tighter
header-to-plot gap, one less line of standfirst, or a hair more width on the clamp) but it has to be
fixed deliberately, and it says the story frame's real constraint is `block ink height ≤ 979px`, not
`plot aspect ∈ range`.

## Recommendation

1. **Centre the block. Do not centre the plot alone.** V1 over V2 on both types, and the reason is
   not balance — it is that the header must stay attached to the chart it describes. V2's 450px hole
   between the source line and the plot's top edge is a worse defect than the one the exercise set
   out to fix. **No per-type split: both types want the same variant.**
2. **Centre it on the platform's band, not on the page.** `269 + (979 − blockHeight) / 2` rather
   than `(1920 − blockHeight) / 2` — 8 to 10 points of frame height higher than the page's own
   centre. On a bare page that looks slightly top-weighted; on the device it is the only one of the
   four whose title, plot and axis are all clear of the interface. If a single number has to be
   written into a size table, it is this one, and it is a platform fact rather than a taste.
3. **Carry the band as a SIZE budget too, not only as a position.** The histogram's block is 11px
   too tall for it today. A story-format layout that cannot state `block ink height ≤ 979px` will
   keep producing compositions that are centred and still covered.
4. **This does not replace the furniture answer, it composes with it.** `PORTRAIT-VERDICT.md` found
   that B0 is a correct chart in a broken frame and that the words are what finish the frame. Both
   remain true: centring makes the leftover legible as a margin, and the furnished arm spends it.
   The two are answers to different halves — where the block sits, and what fills it.

## What this does not close

- **Nothing was looked at on a phone, and no chrome was drawn over any render.** The safe areas are
  applied as arithmetic against published numbers, not as a rendered overlay on a device. That is
  the weakest link in the recommendation and it is cheap to close: one screenshot of one of these
  PNGs in a real story composer would settle it.
- **TikTok's numbers are not TikTok's.** They are the third-party consensus, and TikTok explicitly
  says the zone shrinks with caption length. Any rule written from them should be written from
  Meta's published band, which is stricter and inside them.
- **Only the bare arms were recomposed.** The furnished arms (`h-b-capped-furnished`,
  `l-b-capped-furnished`) and the phone-typeset one (`h-b2-story-type`) fill much more of the frame
  and may not want any shift at all — `h-b2` is already 16.2% plot on a full frame. Where the
  centring rule stops applying because the frame is full is not established here.
- **The line's source credit stays under the bottom chrome in every arm**, because it is pinned to
  the frame's bottom margin by the seed's own discipline. A credit is the cheapest thing to lose,
  but "pinned to the bottom margin" and "the bottom 35% belongs to the platform" are two rules that
  contradict each other on this frame and nothing here resolves it.
- **Nothing is wired into any production component.** One runner, seven renders, this file and the
  generated table. `skills/` and every beat under `proof/` are untouched.
