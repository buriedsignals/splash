# the-stack-gives-back-the-total-it-hides

- kind: imported
- name: A stack states its total outside itself, in a register the segments do not use
- applies: the beat draws bars of two or more segments whose sum a reader would want
- draws: value, annot
- priority: 8
- evidence: 100-datavizproject-com-data-type-viz23
- evidence: informationisbeautiful-net-visualizations-how-much-do-music-artists-ea
- detect: every bar's total appears on the delivered plate, outside the bar, in a different register

## The rule

A stack hides its own total. Give it back.

## The evidence

`100.datavizproject.com`'s viz23 states rule and reason together: *"a stack hides its own total: the
reader has to add. Printing the total **beyond the stack's end, in a register distinct from the
segments' own**, gives back the number the geometry took away — and printing the INCREASE rather than
the later value means no segment's number has to be subtracted from another either."* Its sibling
viz1 does the same: *"22 and 33 sit above their columns in bold … the part **and** the whole are both
readable without arithmetic."*

Information is Beautiful reaches the same end from a table rather than from a chart: the currency
figures in each row let the bar *"be checkable against its own row, which is what makes the bar an
argument rather than a decoration."*

## What the pair agrees on, and where it differs

They differ in where the total comes from — printed past the stack, or already standing in the row.
They agree on the thing that matters: **a stack that leaves the reader to add is a stack that has
taken a number away and not given it back.**

## The second half of viz23's sentence, which is a rule of its own

If the stack is `[level, growth]` rather than `[level, later level]`, then no segment's number has to
be subtracted from another either — the reader gets the start, the change and the total without doing
any arithmetic at all. Where a beat has a choice of what to stack, that is the choice.
