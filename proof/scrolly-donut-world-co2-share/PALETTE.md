**TWO REFERENCES DISAGREE ABOUT HOW A PAST IS DRAWN, AND THE BASE HAS TO CHOOSE.**
Ferdio's #57 — this form's own reference — greys the earlier arc: *"the past is neutral and the
present is accented"*. Ferdio's #3, the lollipop's reference, tints it: *"not grey, not a second hue:
the same hue, lighter"*. Same archive, same series, two answers to one question.

The filed treatment `two-states-of-one-measure-are-one-hue-at-two-chromas` carries **three
independent desks** — Datawrapper, Statista, and #3 — against this record's one. So the tint wins on
evidence, and the disagreement is recorded here rather than quietly resolved.

But evidence is not the only test, and a donut is not a lollipop: the two arcs here are
**concentric**, a few pixels apart, and two chromas of one hue that a reader cannot separate is worse
than a grey they can. So the plate **measures** it. The tint has to clear the non-text floor against
the ground AND a separation floor against the accent it sits beside; where it cannot, the plate falls
back to the neutral this reference asks for and says so on its own ladder. The rule the corpus votes
for is the default; the rule the geometry needs is the fallback.

**The flags are refused.** The reference carries identity with a national flag inside each ring, and
its own record says the move "only work[s] for countries". This base ships no flag assets, and a
plate that drew approximations of them would be inventing a fact about a country. Identity is carried
by the name under the ring.
