The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,
`ground: "#FFFFFF"`). `origin: newsroom` says who chose them.

**The reference spends colour on a category, and so does this beat — a different one.** Its four
hundred cars are coloured by origin, three hues, because with four hundred crossing polylines colour
is the only channel that survives. Sixteen countries do not need three hues to stay apart; what they
need is for the reader to find the two the headline is about.

So the category is **derived, and binary**: the countries that clear both of the first two axes'
thresholds, against the rest. The two are the direction's accent at full strength, the other fourteen
a tint of it — `accent-marks-the-thread` and `context-in-neutral-at-the-subject-scale`, which is the
same rule the reference follows with a different number of groups.

Nothing else on the plate is chromatic: the axes, their ticks and their names are steps off the
direction's own ground.
