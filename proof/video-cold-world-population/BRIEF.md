# Beat — La population mondiale a dépassé 8 milliards en 2022 (video)

**Type:** area. **Medium/format:** chart / **video**. **Size:** landscape (1920 × 1080).

Same subject, same frozen series (`../static-world-population/data.csv`, World, 1800–2023) and the same claim as
`proof/static-world-population`: the population passed 8 billion in 2022 (8,02) — the first year at or above it, derived
from the rows, not typed — and reached 8,09 billion in 2023, more than eight times its 1800 level of 0,98 billion. The
years are asserted consecutive; the area is drawn from zero because a population is a level read from its base.

## Why area, not line

The static sibling is an area, zero-based, and a video tells the same subject. The fill here is not a stock (its surface
would be person-years): nothing in the argument reads the surface. What the argument reads is **heights on one scale from
zero**, which the zero-based area guarantees and a fitted line would not.

## The picture — shots, not a page

1. **The title card** (from frame 0, 1.5 s).
2. **The story** — the chart on the whole frame, zero-based; the counter standing in the empty upper left.
3. **A close-up** — the camera magnifies the last years about sixty times, where the overview draws a year a few pixels
   wide, then pulls back.
4. **No end card** — the video ends on the whole surface, the column of copies and the level; the credit on one line
   under the years.

## The choreography

The claim is a ratio, and a ratio is shown by **using the small value as a ruler**. The video fills the surface year by
year while the population counts up. Then the surface steps back to its tint, and the **1800 slice becomes a unit** — a
column in the accent, exactly as tall as the 1800 reading. **Copies of it rise on top of each other**, linear in value,
the count naming each complete copy (« ×1 » … « ×8 »), until the column's top meets the 2023 level at « ×8,2 ». A dashed
**level runs from the column's top to the 2023 reading**, proving the two heights equal. The overview draws 2022 and
2023 a few pixels apart, so the camera **closes in** on them: the 8 billion gridline, the curve crossing it **ringed**
with « 2022 » above, the level ending on the 2023 reading. The camera **pulls back**; the surface returns to full
strength; the column and the level stay.

Every block is the 1800 reading on the plot's own scale, so the column's height at every frame is a true multiple of it.
