The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,
`ground: "#FFFFFF"`). `origin: newsroom` says who chose them.

**One hue, two chromas, and the reference is the third publication to say so.** Ferdio's #3 draws
the earlier state as *"a light tint of the country's hue"* and the later one at full strength — "not
grey, not a second hue: the same hue, lighter", so the pair reads as one subject in two states
rather than as two subjects. That is
`two-states-of-one-measure-are-one-hue-at-two-chromas`, already carried on Datawrapper's and
Statista's evidence; this record makes three desks.

Where the reference gives each country its own hue, this plate cannot: six hues would be a palette
this base does not own (METHOD correction 28). So **the chroma axis is spent on the two dates, and
identity is carried by the names under the pairs** — which is where the reference puts them anyway.
The subject is ringed rather than recoloured, so no channel is spent twice.
