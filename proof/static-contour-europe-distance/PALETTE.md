The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,
`ground: "#FFFFFF"`). `origin: newsroom` says who chose them.

**This beat DECLINES the one grounded convention its sibling map beats take, and the reason is
measured rather than aesthetic.** `water-is-a-tint-not-a-grey` is admissible on the dot map because
*nothing on that plate is measured in blue*, so a blue cannot be misread as a quantity. Here the
quantity **is distance from the water**. Drawing the sea in a blue tint while the contours that
measure distance from it run on the direction's own ramp would put the reference and the measure in
one channel — and on the `creme` direction, whose accent is a navy, they would be the same hue.

So the sea is the direction's bare ground and the land is one step off it. That is not a compromise:
**the coastline is the zero of this field**, and a field whose zero is the edge of the paper is
easier to read than one whose zero is a second colour.

The contour tones are a ramp between the direction's own ground and its own accent, floored against
**the land they are drawn on** rather than against the plate's ground — the land is a frame the
plate's guards cannot see.
