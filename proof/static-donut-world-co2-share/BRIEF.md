# Beat — En 2000 les États-Unis émettaient un quart du CO₂ mondial et la Chine un septième ; en 2023, c'est l'inverse

**Type:** pie and donut (chart). **Medium/format:** chart / **static**. **Size:** landscape
(1920 x 1080), pinned in the front matter above.

The first `pie and donut` beat in this tree.

## It reads the same frozen file as the lollipop, and asks the other question

`proof/static-lollipop-co2-per-person` draws the **level** each person emits. This draws the **share
of one whole** each country holds. Different questions, and on this data they answer differently:
**Russia's share of world CO₂ fell between 2000 and 2023 while the tonnes it emits rose**, because
the whole grew by half.

That is the trap this form sets, and it is the reason the plate prints the tonnes under every ring.
**A share chart that does not carry its own absolute values can be read backwards and stay silent
about it.** The plate also refuses to render if no country among the six actually falls into the
trap — a warning about nothing is not a reading line.

## The claim

**In 2000 the United States emitted 24.4 % of the world's CO₂ and China 14.7 %; in 2023 China emits
32.9 % and the United States 13.3 %.** They swapped. Meanwhile the world's total went from 24.7 to
37.0 Gt — up 50 %.

The plate refuses if the two did not swap or if the whole did not grow by at least a third. The six
countries are the same computed rule the lollipop uses: the largest 2023 totals, derived from
per-person emissions times population, both from the frozen file.

## One full turn is the world, on every ring, at one radius

The reference's own record leaves this open — *"whether the arcs are on a common scale across the
three rings — if they are not, the between-country comparison the layout invites would be false"* —
so this beat closes it rather than inheriting the doubt. Six rings side by side **are** a small
multiple, and a reader will compare them whether or not the scale lets them:
`panels-share-one-scale-or-they-are-not-multiples`, in a radial geometry.

Two floors are measured before the rings are drawn: **the shortest arc on the plate must be at least
three pixels of drawn length** (an arc a reader cannot see is not a share, it is a gap in the ring),
and **the hole must hold its own number**. The ladder spends copy until both are met, and prints the
smallest arc it achieved.

## Two references disagree, and the disagreement is on the record

Ferdio's #57 — this form's own reference — **greys** the earlier arc: *"the past is neutral and the
present is accented"*. Ferdio's #3, the lollipop's reference, **tints** it: *"not grey, not a second
hue: the same hue, lighter"*. Same archive, same series, two answers.

`two-states-of-one-measure-are-one-hue-at-two-chromas` carries three independent desks against this
record's one, so the tint is the default. **But evidence is not the only test, and a donut is not a
lollipop**: here the two arcs are concentric, a few pixels apart, and two chromas of one hue a reader
cannot separate is worse than a grey they can. So the plate measures it — the tint has to clear the
non-text floor against the ground *and* a separation floor against the accent beside it — and where
it cannot, it falls back to this reference's neutral and says which it used on its ladder.

**On all three filed directions the tint failed and the neutral was drawn.** The rule the corpus
votes for is the default; the rule the geometry needs is what ships. The lollipop, whose two marks
are far apart, lifts its tint instead of falling back — the same question, answered by the geometry
each time rather than by a house habit.

## What the reference gave, and what is refused

**Taken:** *draw the earlier value concentric with the later one, so the change is a gap rather than
a second mark.* Two arcs, one ring, one centre.

**Refused, with the reason:** the reference carries identity with a **national flag** inside each
ring, and its own record says the move *"only work[s] for countries"*. This base ships no flag
assets, and a plate that drew approximations of them would be inventing a fact about a country.
Identity is the name under the ring.

`the-subject-is-ringed-not-recoloured` — the chroma axis is already spent on the two dates, so the
subject takes a shape: a hairline around its own number, hugging it rather than sitting at a radius
where it would read as a third track.

## A note on this form's own evidence

One publication, from an archive whose pixel figures this base already refuses to quote — the record
says so itself: *"no direction may be measured from this record"*. Below the floor of two, so the
form files **no family rules of its own**.

## Source

Global Carbon Budget 2025 (CO₂ per person, 2000 and 2023) and Our World in Data's population series
(both years), frozen beside this beat as `data.csv` — 213 countries. Every total on the plate,
including the world's, is summed from that file rather than quoted.
