The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,
`ground: "#FFFFFF"`). `origin: newsroom` says who chose them. This is the palette
`composeDirections` reconciles the three filed directions against; the delivered page is drawn in
whichever direction governs it, never in this one.

**One hue, two chromas, and they are two DATES of one measure.** Every pair on this page draws the
same quantity — tonnes of CO₂ per person — twice, once for 2000 and once for 2023. That is
`two-states-of-one-measure-are-one-hue-at-two-chromas` in its plainest case: the earlier state is a
tint of the later state's own hue, mixed toward the direction's ground only as far as the non-text
floor against that ground allows. Not grey, not a second hue — a pair has to read as one country in
two states, never as two countries.

Where this form's reference gives each country its own hue, this page cannot: six hues would be a
palette this base does not own. The chroma axis is spent on the two dates, so the subject cannot also
take it — `the-subject-is-ringed-not-recoloured` rings China's 2023 head instead.

## What the earlier state actually measures, and the head that was not there

The tint lands at the non-text floor and stays there — **3,10:1 in creme, 3,07:1 in rapport, 3,07:1
in nocturne** against each direction's own ground, against 6,64:1 / 7,09:1 / 10,80:1 for the later
state. That is the tint taken as far toward the ground as it can go while still being a mark a reader
can see, which is the rule; a past nobody can see is not a state.

What it does **not** buy is the head. This beat shipped its dot filled with the very colour its stem
was stroked with — **1,00:1, both states, all three directions** — so a lollipop's one defining mark
was drawn and was invisible, and each pair read as two plain bars. It is the same family fact the
dumbbell pass recorded one commit over, where two marks clamped independently to the same floor on
the same ground came out at 1,023:1 by construction. Here it is simpler and worse: one colour used
twice.

**No colour can fix it**, because the head and the stem are not two things to tell apart — they are
one state, and a head in a different hue would be a second encoding of nothing. So the separation is
geometric and it is measured rather than looked at: every head sits on its own **ground casing**,
2,5 reader pixels wide and drawn `non-scaling-stroke` so it is the same hairline at 320 px and at
1600. The casing is the ground, so what it has to clear is the stem it interrupts — which is the
stem's own contrast against the ground, already listed above: **3,10 / 3,07 / 3,07** for the earlier
stem and **6,64 / 7,09 / 10,80** for the later one. All six clear the 3:1 non-text floor, and the
component throws if any of them ever stops doing so.

## The value labels are never the mark's colour

`references/types/lollipop.md` names this as the type's own previously-shipped failure: a saturated
accent that reads fine on a thin stem, carried into the value label beside the dot "for visual
consistency", measured well under 4,5:1 as running text. This page shipped the subject's own 2023
figure in the accent. It measured 6,64 / 7,09 / 10,80:1 — legible, and still the wrong rule, because
what decides an element's colour here is whether it is text or a mark, not what it sits next to. All
twelve printed values are in the direction's ink now. China is identified by the ring on its head and
by its name in the axis, which are marks and a name, not a value.

## No opacity anywhere near a clamped ink

The per-cent change under each name was painted at `opacity: 0.8` over an ink `deriveFurniture` had
already clamped to exactly the 4,5:1 text floor. What reached the reader was **3,89:1 in creme,
3,90:1 in rapport, 5,04:1 in nocturne** — and **4,33:1 / 4,41:1** where the subject's accent carried
it. An opacity applied after a clamp spends the clamp, and the page's own stylesheet cannot see it.
There is no opacity on any ink on this page; a quieter register is asked for as a register.

## The reader's yardstick is not the accent

The control lays one country's two levels flat across all six pairs. Those references are drawn in
the direction's **ink**, never in either state's colour: the accent already carries the later state
and the subject, and a hue that also meant "this is the country I chose" would leave the reader no
way to tell the page's claim from their own question. Each reference carries a ground casing under
its dashes, because a reference is laid down to be read **where it crosses** — and it crosses stems,
heads and gridlines, each of which the component measures the dash and the casing against before it
draws anything.

Nothing else on the page is chromatic. The axis labels, the baseline, the gridlines and the tooltip
are steps off the direction's own ground, computed by `deriveFurniture` at render time and never
written here as a literal.
