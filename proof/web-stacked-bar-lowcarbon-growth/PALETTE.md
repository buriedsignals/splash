The answer recorded for this beat: the newsroom's own house colours, as they stand in
`skills/splash/assets/root-template/NEWSROOM.example.md` (`brandColor: "#0B7A75"`,

whichever direction governs it, never in this one.

## One hue, five chromas, and why five and not six

This page does not partition one quantity into two states of itself — the reasoning the forty
identical copies of this file carry, and it is not this beat's. It partitions **one quantity into
five named parts**: the low-carbon electricity of sixteen countries, by source, in a stacking order
that is identical in every row. Five parts of one whole is one hue at five chromas —
`two-states-of-one-measure-are-one-hue-at-two-chromas` generalised, not a second hue imported per
source, which would make five arbitrary colours out of one measured one.

**The number of chromas is a measurement, not a preference.** A ramp in one hue lives between the
accent's own contrast against the ground and the WCAG non-text floor of 3:1 — 6,64:1 on `creme`,
7,09:1 on `rapport`, 10,80:1 on `nocturne`. That interval is all the room there is, and dividing it
into six leaves 1,165 – 1,189:1 between neighbours in every direction, which is not a difference a
reader sees. Five leaves what the tables below record. `types/stacked-bar.md` reaches the same place
from the other side, and says so as a hard limit: past roughly five series a stack *"turns into an
unreadable ribbon — group the smallest into 'Other' rather than adding a sixth colour"*. So the
file's bioenergy column and its own "other renewables" column — whose largest value anywhere is
Italy's 5,67 TWh — are drawn as one band.

## What the first form of this ramp actually painted, and why it had to go

The first form mixed the accent toward the ground on a **linear** ladder (0,2 to 1,0) and then
floor-adjusted anything that fell under 3:1. Measured on `creme`, its six steps stood

> 1,400 · 1,415 · **1,079** · **1,015** · **1,016**

apart. The top three bands were one colour to any eye — and not by accident: three independent
clamps against the same floor against the same ground land on the same value **by construction**,
which is the defect this branch has already written down twice (1,023:1 on one beat, 1,000:1 on a
lollipop). The ladder is now walked in **contrast** instead, geometrically from the accent's own
contrast down to the floor, each step being the first mix that reaches its target — so the last band
lands ON the floor rather than under it, and no two land on the same place. The separation between
neighbours is then measured and **refused** under 1,2:1.

## The pointed-at band, searched off its own fill

A segment answers by darkening **from the colour it is already painted in**, and the dose is
searched rather than set: a fixed dose was refused at 1,104:1 on `nocturne` elsewhere on this branch,
and a `brightness()` filter lightens on a light ground and on a dark one alike. The search walks the
mix toward the direction's ink until the result stands at least 1,4:1 from the fill it replaces AND
still clears 3:1 against the ground — and it refuses rather than approximate.

## The rail, and it borrows rather than invents

The rail under each bar is drawn in **its own band's tone** — the only cue tying the lane to the
stack above it, and a colour already measured above rather than a sixth one introduced for the
control. Measured on the painted pixel at 1440 x 900, with « l'éolien » chosen: 4,51:1 against the
cream ground, 5,72:1 against the nocturne ground, 4,66:1 against the white one.

## The control's own chrome is not drawn here

The chosen pill is the trunk's `control-chrome.ts` without variation: a wash of the direction's
accent at 22 % into its own ground, a 1,5 px ring of the accent at full strength, and the words from
`--muted` to `--ink`. Measured on the painted pixel of this beat's own pages, option chosen by a real
click, after the 120 ms transition: wash **1,409 / 1,619 / 1,410:1** against the ground, ring
**6,637 / 10,797 / 7,090:1**, chosen words on the wash **14,49 / 10,98 / 14,89:1**, unchosen words
**6,13 / 7,28 / 6,19:1**. The ink slab is gone, and it is gone in one place for every beat.
