Between 1990 and 2024, 26 of the European Union's 27 member states cut the carbon dioxide they emit per person. Drawn as lines, that is all you can see: the median of the 27 falls from 8.1 tonnes to 5.3.

Falling is not the same as low, and a line chart cannot tell you who is who. Sorted, 2024 reads differently: Luxembourg emits 10.5 tonnes per person, Malta 3.2 — a factor of 3.3 between the top and the bottom of the same union.

Join the two ends and a third answer appears: the largest cut in the union belongs to the country still at the top. Luxembourg shed 20.5 tonnes per person and is still the highest of the 27. Croatia is the one member state emitting more than in 1990, by 0.03.

Take the names away and the ranking turns into a crowd: 25 of the 27 sit within two tonnes of the median and 13 within one. Four charts, one column of numbers — and which question you asked decided what you saw.
