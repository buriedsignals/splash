## 3. The architecture, in the terms that decide answers

**The twin is Tom Vaillant's self-contained-skill method.** A skill stays copy-pasteable on its own,
so **helpers are duplicated, never imported across skills** — enforced by `no-cross-skill-imports.test.ts`.

This decides every "how do we make X true for 23 types" question. The answer is **never** a shared
module. It is: *the same change, made identically in each craft skill, with a WALKING parity test
proving the copies stay in step.* The owner corrected me on this mid-session when I said "engine",
which is the original Splash's shape — the word alone was proposing the wrong architecture.

**Why duplication is safe here now:** `render-still-parity.test.ts` and `video-helper-parity.test.ts`
WALK the tree and compare function by function, so a new copy is guarded the moment it lands.
`helper-parity.test.ts`'s hand-written import list is the standing counter-example — it turned the
suite red for a *correct* change, and two agents kept a dead export alive to satisfy it.

**Four invariants no work may trade away:**

1. Nothing renders in a colour, value or default nobody chose; a missing input **throws** naming what it looked for.
2. Every number a reader sees is **derived** from the beat's own frozen data, never typed.
3. A beat's inputs and outputs live in the beat's own folder.
4. **A guard that cannot go red is worse than no guard** — every guard ships with the mutation that reddens it, run in a copy outside the tree.

## 6. The open design question: portrait, and mobile

The probe rendered a histogram at three sizes. **Portrait came back mechanically perfect and
editorially destroyed**: 0 clipped, 0 collisions, 84% fill — the best of the three — while the plot's
aspect went 2.35:1 → 0.54:1 and the tallest bar 4.2:1 → **18.4:1**. A right-skewed distribution reads
as one column beside nine slivers. Confirmed on a line: a one-third fall draws as a cliff.
**No measurement catches it.**

The owner: *"Le portrait ressemble à la même problématique que mobile."* He is right — they are one
question.

**The hypothesis being tested** (probe in flight): a frame's aspect and a plot's aspect are different
things, and today the plot fills the frame. Instead — **the type declares the aspect range its
geometry supports; the plot takes the nearest aspect inside it; the leftover height goes to editorial
furniture** (a larger title, a developed subtitle, unfolded annotations, a flat legend). The frame is
9:16; the plot is not. Plus, for types with a twin form, **transpose rather than scale** — vertical
bars become horizontal bars in portrait. And where a type cannot honestly enter a format, **refuse
the format and say so** rather than deliver a distorted chart.

If it holds, its home is the **type sheets** — each already carries "what it is for / when NOT to use
it / the one thing that goes wrong"; an aspect range is a fourth line.

## 7. The method lessons, which cost the most to learn

- **A guard that cannot go red is worse than none.** `render-still-parity.test.ts` shipped comparing
  *signatures* instead of bodies — it took the `{` of an argument's inline type as the body's — so it
  was inert for every function with a destructured or object-typed parameter. Which is most of them.
  Found only by mutating a real `wrap` and watching it stay green.
- **A guard maintained by remembering stops covering things**, and can make people write worse code:
  `helper-parity`'s list forced two agents to keep a dead export alive.
- **The instrument lies as often as the code.** Five times this session: a scanner crying drift over
  reformatting; a script marking four verified-sound PNGs as superseded; a hover probe measuring a
  response radius without ever measuring the disc; a mutation harness reading `tail`'s exit code and
  printing GREEN over a screen of FAILs; a parity guard comparing a number to itself.
- **A sampled probe is not a continuous one.** 25 scroll positions measured 25/25 correct while the
  owner watched visuals arrive at the wrong moment.
- **Looking finds what tests cannot.** Every serious defect this session came from opening the
  artifact: 6 of 13 map points cropped, a label naming a height its column had not reached, a
  crossfade painting both halves of a handover, a portrait that destroys a distribution.
- **Report only what you have read.** Two agents retracted numbers they had stated before the output
  landed — one was right by luck and said so anyway.
