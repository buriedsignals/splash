Every earthquake of magnitude 4.0 and above the USGS catalogued in 2024: 14,057 of them, one dot each. Nobody drew the plate boundaries — the dots did. What dots cannot do is let you count: on the busiest rims, hundreds sit on top of one another.

Binned into 156 equal hexagons, the pile-up becomes countable — and it is more concentrated than the dots let you guess: 10 of the 156 cells hold half of all 14,057 events. The busiest, ringed, holds 1,374, off Fiji and Tonga.

How many is not how strong. Only 28 of the year's earthquakes reached magnitude 6.5, and sized by the energy that implies they pick out different coasts. The largest, magnitude 7.5: the 2024 Noto Peninsula, Japan Earthquake.

Shade the same 156 hexagons by their strongest event instead of their count and the ring moves: the cell holding that magnitude 7.5, off Japan and Russia, is only the 7th busiest of the year — 518 events against the leader's 1,374.
