# Beat 1 -- rate, not raw count

**Proves**: that the incident rate per resident, not the raw count, decides which district has the
worst safety record.

**Proof hierarchy**: the rate first -- five bars, sorted by incidents per 100,000 residents, zero
baseline. Sul is the longest bar, not Centro. Centro's raw count (412, the highest in the city) is
named directly beside its own bar, so a reader who already knows the headline's claim can see
exactly why it does not survive dividing by population, without the chart repeating the false
claim itself.

**One accent**: Sul's bar -- the subject the honest reading names. Every other bar, including
Centro's, stays in the newsroom's muted ink so no bar competes with the one the argument is about.

**Anti-patterns for this case**: do not accent Centro (it is the highest raw count, not the highest
rate -- accenting it would restate the very error this beat exists to correct); do not truncate the
rate axis (a bar's length carries the value, so zero stays the baseline); do not omit Centro's raw
count entirely (the beat's job is to show why the intuitive reading is wrong, not to hide the
number that produced it).

**Limit to carry**: residents is a single population figure per district; it is not confirmed to be
drawn from the same year as the incident counts.
