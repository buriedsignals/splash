# Japan's worst bear year on record was a Tohoku year

Japan's Ministry of the Environment closed the books on fiscal 2025 with a sentence it had never
written before. In the Cabinet Secretariat briefing of 19 May 2026, prepared for the fourth meeting
of the government's bear-damage task force, the ministry's own summary line reads:
「令和７年度は、出没情報件数、人身被害者数、死亡者数が過去最多となった。」 — *in fiscal 2025,
sighting reports, casualties and deaths all reached the highest figures on record.*

The three numbers behind that sentence are 50,776 sighting reports, 216 separate incidents, and
238 people hurt, of whom 13 died. The year before, the same table records 20,513 sightings, 82
incidents, 85 casualties and 3 deaths. Fiscal 2023, the previous bad autumn, records 24,348
sightings and 219 casualties but only 6 deaths. Nothing in the published series comes close to
fiscal 2025 on the count that matters most.

What the national total hides is where those 238 people were. The ministry publishes the casualty
figures broken down by prefecture and by month, as a preliminary table it refreshes through the
year — 「Ｒ０７年度におけるクマの人身被害件数［速報値］」. Read down its 計 column and the record
year turns out to be a regional one. The six prefectures of Tohoku — Aomori, Iwate, Miyagi, Akita,
Yamagata and Fukushima — account for 158 of the 238 casualties — two thirds of the national figure.

Akita alone accounts for 67. That is more than a quarter of everyone hurt by a bear in Japan last
year, in a prefecture of roughly 900,000 people, and 37 of those 67 were hurt in October alone.
Iwate accounts for 40 and for 5 of the 13 deaths — more deaths than any other prefecture. Akita
records 4. Between them, Akita and Iwate account for 107 casualties, nine of the thirteen deaths,
and 45 per cent of the national casualty count.

The prefecture-level capture figures in the same briefing point the same way. Akita permitted 2,690
bears to be taken in fiscal 2025 against 382 the year before; the six Tohoku prefectures together
permitted 8,849, which the briefing calls 過去最高の許可捕獲数 — the highest permitted take on
record. Akita's own published population estimate for its bears, released the same year, is 3,900.

Outside Tohoku the picture thins fast. Niigata records 17 casualties, Nagano 16, Gunma 12, Hokkaido
6, Toyama 6. Seventeen of the 39 prefectures in the table record a clean zero for the whole year. Eight
prefectures — Fukuoka, Saga, Nagasaki, Kumamoto, Oita, Miyazaki, Kagoshima and Okinawa — do not
appear in the ministry's table at all.

## What the figures are, and are not

The ministry marks the table 速報値, preliminary. Its own note reads 「※都道府県から聞き取った速報
値です。今後、数値が変更することがあります。」 — *these are preliminary figures gathered from the
prefectures; they may change.* A second note, 「※被害者数に死亡者数含む。」, states that the
casualty count already includes the deaths, so the two columns must never be added.

The absent eight prefectures are not zeroes. The ministry does not say why they are missing; the
neighbouring Chugoku and Shikoku prefectures, which do appear, are all zero, and the usual reading
is that the missing eight have no established bear population to report on. A reader should treat
those eight as *not reported* rather than as *none*, and this piece does.

One further caution belongs on any national comparison drawn from this material. The briefing's own
footnote reads 「※北海道は出没件数の公表は行っていないため、出没件数は北海道以外の都府県の合計。
人身被害、死亡者数は全都道府県の合計。」 — Hokkaido does not publish sighting counts, so the
national sighting figure excludes it, while the casualty and death figures cover every prefecture.
The casualty table used here is therefore complete for the prefectures it lists; the 50,776 sighting
figure is not.

Experts quoted in the same briefing expect the pressure to continue: 「令和７年秋の大量出没時に捕獲
できなかった個体が、一定数、市街地等の周辺に残存している可能性がある」 — a number of the animals
that were not caught during the autumn 2025 surge may still be living around towns. Twelve
prefectures told the ministry that April 2026 sightings were already running above the same month a
year earlier, and all six Tohoku prefectures are on that list.
