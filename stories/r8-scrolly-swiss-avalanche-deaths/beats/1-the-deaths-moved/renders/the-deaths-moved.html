Since the winter of 1936/37 the SLF has recorded every avalanche in Switzerland that killed somebody. There have been 1 406 of them, and they have killed 2 146 people — an average of 24.4 a winter across 88 winters.

Each dot is one avalanche, at the point where it started. GR and VS hold 65 per cent of the dead between them.

The SLF records where each person was, not only that they died. Inside or beside a building, on a road, a railway or a ski run, the terrain is controlled: somebody is responsible for it, and it can be closed, blasted or built against.

On tour or off-piste, nobody is. 1 620 of the deaths are on that side of the line and 514 on the other. 12 sit on neither and are counted as neither.

In the first 20 winters on record, 247 of 478 deaths were in controlled terrain. In the last 20, 8 of 442.

The toll itself barely moved. What moved was where it fell — and 1950/51, the worst winter on record at 99 dead, 95 of them indoors or on a road, is the kind of winter Switzerland has not had since.

The national avalanche bulletin has forecast a danger level since long before most of these accidents. On the 323 fatal avalanches that carry one, the level most often forecast was 3, considerable: 209 of them. Level 4, high, accounts for 14.

The SLF's own warning about this reading, verbatim: “this graph does not correspond to an individual’s risk because only the absolute numbers of accidents are shown without reference to the sizes of the risk populations surveyed in each category.” Most people are out on a considerable day because most days are considerable.
