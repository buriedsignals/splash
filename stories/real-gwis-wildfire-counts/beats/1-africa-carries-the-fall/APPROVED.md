Approved. The stack's roof falls visibly from its labelled 1,148,499 peak in 2015 to the 626,326
the gutter names at 2025; Africa is the only accented band, on the flat baseline, labelled with its
own number and its 65% share; the five comparison bands are each named and each carries its own
2025 value, so the two-thin ones (Europe at 18,295, North America at 25,408) are readable as numbers
even where the band itself is a few pixels thick. The period is stated. 2026 is stated as excluded,
with the reason, on the face of the chart.

Two limitations recorded rather than waved through:

1. **The chart cannot say what a fire IS.** Every number on it is a count of detected fire events.
   Most of Africa's count is seasonal savanna and agricultural burning, and the explainer the
   article quotes — deaths, evacuations, burned area — is about a different quantity. The chart
   does not use the word "worst" anywhere, the note says "fire events recorded", and the caveat
   travels with the hand-over. Nothing in the toolchain raised this; it came from reading the
   article against the column.

2. **There is no denominator, and none is available.** A count per continent is not a rate. The
   frozen table carries no area or population column, so the raw-count downgrade in the grounding
   check never fires here — the check looks for a denominator-shaped column in the SAME table, and
   for this dataset the denominator is a different dataset. Recorded in `SUBJECTS.md` as an angle
   a second dataset could take, not one this one can.
