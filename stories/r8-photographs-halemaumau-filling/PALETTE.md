# The palette for this story

`proposePalette` was run against the storyboard's own `subject` and `takeaway`, with
`surface: "static"` and `destination: "screen"` — the answers gate 2b and gate 2c recorded — and
found **no subject convention**. It said so with its own reach attached rather than silently: the
convention table reads English, French, Greek and Arabic, and *"U+02BB is a script it holds no word
of, so 'no convention applies' here means 'none could be read', not 'none exists'."* U+02BB is the
ʻokina in *Halemaʻumaʻu*. There is in any case no reader-held colour convention for a volcanic
crater, so the newsroom's own colours lead, which is what the proposal recommended.

Both options were measured against the house ground `#16191B` and both clear the 3:1 non-text floor
(WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11): `#D4A853` at **8.01:1** and `#5B8A8A` at **4.58:1**. The journalist answered
**option 1**, the house colours, so `origin: journalist`.

**What an image beat actually spends these on.** The ground and nothing else, in practice.
`image-beat` draws no marks — the evidence is three photographs — and
`references/image-discipline.md` is explicit that the closed palette governs the furniture this
skill invents and never the colours inside a photograph. `deriveFurniture("#16191B")` derives the
title ink, the caption ink, the muted credit grey and the `grid` that fills the letterbox bars. The
dark ground is the right one here for a reason beyond the house: two of these three photographs are
of a night-time lava fountain, and a dark surround keeps a reader's eye adapted to them instead of
flaring around a white frame.

**The accent is recorded and, on this frame, drawn once** — the rule under the quoted figure line,
which is the only mark on the frame that is not a photograph, a caption or a credit. It is recorded
in full so a later beat on this story, or a scrolly carrying this one, takes the newsroom's own
colour rather than inventing one.
