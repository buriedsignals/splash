# Kīlauea is refilling the hole it blew in itself

Source article: *Volcano Watch — When will Halemaʻumaʻu fill with lava?*, U.S. Geological Survey
Hawaiian Volcano Observatory, published 16 July 2026,
<https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/news/volcano-watch-when-will-halemaumau-fill-lava>.
Public domain. Retrieved 23 August 2026.

The following paragraphs are the observatory's own published prose, quoted as the desk received it.

> USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory (HVO) staff are often asked, "Will lava erupted during the
> ongoing Kīlauea summit eruption in Halemaʻumaʻu eventually flow out of the caldera? And if so,
> when?" The answer is complicated.

> The topography of Kaluapele, Kīlauea volcano's summit caldera, is stepped—a series of ledges at
> different elevations. The deepest part, which has been covered with lava flows during the ongoing
> episodic eruption in Halemaʻumaʻu that began in December 2024, collapsed downwards in 2018.

> The volume of the void left within the caldera by the 2018 collapse was approximately 0.20 cubic
> miles (825 million cubic meters) and the bottom of Halemaʻumaʻu crater settled at an elevation of
> 1,696 feet (517 meters) above sea level.

> Five eruptions within Halemaʻumaʻu between 2020 and 2023 filled in about 25% of the 2018 collapse
> volume, raising the crater floor by 1,313 feet (400 meters) to an elevation of 3,009 feet
> (917 meters).

> An additional 35% of the 2018 collapsed volume has been filled in by the ongoing episodic
> fountaining eruption in Halemaʻumaʻu over the past year and a half, and the crater floor is an
> additional 295 feet (90 meters) higher.

> In total, 60% of the 2018 collapsed volume has been filled in, raising the crater floor by
> 1,608 feet (490 meters). Assuming that the ongoing Kīlauea summit eruption continues at the same
> rate is has over the past year (0.04 cubic miles or 183 million cubic meters) for the next year
> and a half, the 2018 collapse could be almost totally filled in by early 2028.

> Another important factor to consider is the elevation of the vents within Halemaʻumaʻu, which
> remain 215 feet (65 meters) lower than the crater rim.

> Also remember, as the lava fountaining episodes at the summit of Kilauea have demonstrated,
> eruption rates vary over time.

> Kīlauea has been erupting episodically within the summit caldera since December 23, 2024. Its
> USGS Volcano Alert level is ADVISORY.

## What the desk wants on the page

Three photographs, run as a sequence, that show the filling actually happening: the observatory's
own before-and-after webcam frame from the first and the fiftieth fountaining episode, the July
overflight of the resurfaced western crater floor, and the panorama shot during episode 53 on
12 August. Photographs, not a chart — the argument here is a place changing shape, and the desk has
no per-episode volume series to plot.

The picture desk pulled the manifest straight off the USGS media pages this morning and did not
tidy it. USGS supplies a caption and a usage line but no alt text, so the alt column arrived empty;
two of the three were written here before hand-over and the third was not, because nobody could
agree how to describe a single file that contains two frames stacked. One of the three carries no
photographer at all — the observatory credits it only as "USGS webcam images." The place name is
spelled three different ways across the observatory's own captions, and the article above spells
the volcano itself two ways.
