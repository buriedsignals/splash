# Rabies

*World Health Organization — fact sheet, 5 June 2024.
Source page: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies
Retrieved 23 August 2026.*

## Key facts

Rabies is a serious public health problem in over 150 countries and territories, mainly in Asia and
Africa. It is a viral, zoonotic, neglected tropical disease that causes tens of thousands of deaths
annually, with 40% being children under 15. Dog bites and scratches cause 99% of the human rabies
cases, and can be prevented through dog vaccination and bite prevention. Once the virus infects the
central nervous system and clinical symptoms appear, rabies is fatal in 100% of cases.

WHO and its global partners aim to end human deaths from dog-mediated rabies through a comprehensive
One Health approach promoting mass dog vaccination, ensuring access to PEP, health worker training,
improved surveillance, and bite prevention through community awareness.

## Overview

Rabies is present on all continents except Antarctica. Globally there are an estimated 59 000 deaths
from rabies annually; however, due to underreporting, documented case numbers often differ from the
estimate.

Rabies, a neglected tropical disease (NTD), predominantly affects marginalized populations. Although
effective human vaccines and immunoglobulins exist for rabies, these are often inaccessible or
unaffordable to those in need. As of 2018, the average estimated cost of rabies post-exposure
prophylaxis (PEP) was US$ 108 (along with travel costs and loss of income), which can be a financial
burden on those earning US$ 1–2 per person, daily.

The global cost of rabies is estimated to be around US$ 8.6 billion per year including lost lives and
livelihoods, medical care and associated costs, as well as uncalculated psychological trauma.

In the Americas, where dog-mediated rabies is mostly controlled, hematophagous (blood-feeding) bats
are now the primary source of human rabies.

## WHO response

Rabies is included in WHO's 2021–2030 Roadmap for the global control of NTDs, which sets regional,
progressive targets for the global strategic plan to end human deaths from dog-mediated rabies by
2030 (see also: Zero by 30).

## The data behind this story

WHO Global Health Observatory indicator **NTD_RAB2 — "Reported number of human rabies deaths"**,
downloaded 23 August 2026 from `https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/NTD_RAB2` (the Observatory's own
OData endpoint; the legacy Athena CSV endpoint documented on who.int now returns an HTML page rather
than a CSV). The payload is 2 919 country-year rows covering 2010 to 2024, last stamped by WHO on
9 September 2025. For 2024 it holds 195 country rows.

Every country-year in the file carries one of three states, and the difference between them is the
story: a reported number, a reported zero, or the literal string "No data" — a country that filed
nothing at all. Of the 195 countries in the 2024 row, 94 filed nothing, 44 reported zero deaths, and
57 reported at least one. Their reported deaths add up to 3 021.

India, which reported 395 rabies deaths in 2023 and 971 in 2015, is one of the countries whose 2024
cell reads "No data". So are China and Pakistan.
