# Τα σχολεία κλείνουν παντού εκτός από την Αττική

Between 2020 and 2026 the number of schools operating in Greece fell in every one of the country's thirteen administrative regions, according to figures the education ministry released to us in response to a written request. The decline is not evenly spread: the regions of Western Macedonia, the Peloponnese and Central Greece each lost more than a tenth of their schools over the six years, while Attica — which contains Athens and roughly a third of the country's pupils — lost barely three per cent of its own.

The ministry attributes the closures to a combination of falling birth rates and a long-running consolidation programme that merges small rural schools into larger units, a programme that officials describe as a way of guaranteeing that every pupil has access to specialist teaching in subjects such as physics and modern languages, and that critics in the affected communities describe instead as the quiet withdrawal of the state from places that have already lost their bank branch, their post office and, in several documented cases in the mountainous north, their only doctor.

Attica has the most schools of any region.
