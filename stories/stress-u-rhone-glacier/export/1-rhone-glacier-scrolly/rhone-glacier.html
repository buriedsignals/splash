In 1990 the Rhone glacier covered 1.82 square kilometres. The faint line ahead of you is the whole record: 8 measurements, one every 5 years, 1990 to 2025. Scroll, and the reading you are standing on fills in.

1995: 1.71 square kilometres. 0.11 square kilometres gone since 1990, 6.0 per cent off the 1990 glacier so far.

2000: 1.55 square kilometres. 0.16 square kilometres gone since 1995, 14.8 per cent off the 1990 glacier so far.

2005: 1.55 square kilometres — the same figure as 2000, to the last digit. For 5 years the line runs flat. The monitoring network puts that down to a run of heavy winters rather than to any recovery; the table itself records only that the number did not move, and says nothing about the 5 years in between two measurements.

2010: 1.31 square kilometres. The retreat resumes the moment the pause ends — 0.24 square kilometres gone since 2005, 28.0 per cent off the 1990 glacier so far.

2015: 1.09 square kilometres. 0.22 square kilometres gone since 2010, 40.1 per cent off the 1990 glacier so far.

2020: 0.86 square kilometres. 0.23 square kilometres gone since 2015, 52.7 per cent off the 1990 glacier so far.

2025: 0.61 square kilometres. 66.5 per cent of the 1990 glacier is gone — the shaded band is all of it. The volume fell further still, from 0.094 to 0.019 cubic kilometres, 79.8 per cent: the ice thinned as well as retreated.
