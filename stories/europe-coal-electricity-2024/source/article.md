# Europe's coal power is now a handful-of-countries problem

Coal still kept the lights on in 35 European countries in 2024 — but not evenly. A small
cluster of countries accounts for the overwhelming majority of the continent's coal-fired
electricity, while most of the rest generate only a token amount.

Russia alone generated more coal electricity than the next eight countries combined. Add
Italy, the UK and Germany, and four countries account for roughly seven in ten megawatt-hours
of coal power generated in Europe in 2024. Twenty-five countries, by contrast, each contributed
less than one percent of the continental total.

The story is not that coal is gone from Europe's grid — it plainly isn't — but that what remains
has concentrated into a short list of holdouts, rather than being spread thinly across the
continent.
