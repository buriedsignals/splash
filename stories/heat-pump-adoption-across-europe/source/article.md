# Europe’s heat-pump gap is narrowing, but the Nordics still lead

Heat pumps spread rapidly across ten European countries between 2021 and 2025,
according to a synthetic dataset prepared to test the Splash visual-production
workflow. The average share of households with a heat pump rose from 20.5% to
29.8% over the period.

The Netherlands made the largest gain, climbing 18 percentage points from 8%
of households in 2021 to 26% in 2025. Poland rose by 12 points and France by
11. Every country in the sample recorded an increase.

That convergence has not erased the divide. Norway and Sweden ended 2025 at
64% and 62% respectively, while the United Kingdom remained at 9% and Spain at
12%. Finland, at 53%, was the only other country above half of households.

“The movement matters, but so does the distance left to travel,” said Dr Mira
Vale, a fictional energy-policy researcher interviewed for this test. “A
country can be accelerating and still remain far behind the leaders.”

The figures support two related readings: adoption is rising throughout the
sample, and the 2025 spread remains wide despite that shared direction.
