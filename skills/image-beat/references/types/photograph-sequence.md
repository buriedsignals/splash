# Photograph sequence

## What it's for

A run of the journalist's own photographs, read in the order they were taken or the order the
argument needs, each carrying its own caption and its own credit. It answers "what did this look
like" — the question no chart and no map can answer, because the evidence IS the photograph rather
than a measurement drawn from it. Reach for it when the reporting produced images that carry the
finding themselves: a place before and after, a document, a face, a queue, a damaged span.

The anatomy is one consistent letterboxed box per photograph, the journalist's own words beneath,
and nothing invented in between — no automatic description, no crop that decides what matters, no
recomposition. `image-beat/SKILL.md` holds it in full; this sheet exists so the survey at movement
④ has something to enumerate and the treatment question at movement ⑩ has a candidate to name.

## When not to reach for it

One photograph is an illustration, not a sequence, and it belongs in the article rather than in a
beat of its own — `imageBeatLayout` refuses fewer than two. A set whose photographs do not carry
alt text and a credit is refused too, and rightly: a photograph without alt text is unusable to a
screen reader and one without a credit is a rights problem, and neither can be derived.

Photographs that merely decorate a numerical finding are not this type either — if the argument is
carried by the numbers, the numbers should be drawn, and the photograph is furniture around them.
And a sequence long enough that a reader scrolls past the argument wants the scroll-driven form
(`image` × `scrolly`), where prose cards advance over a fixed frame, rather than a taller still.

The IMAGE track of the scrolly seed, which is this type placed under a travelling prose card
