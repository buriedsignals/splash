Europe's heat-pump divide is narrowing, but the Nordics still lead

Heat pumps spread rapidly across ten European countries between 2021 and 2025, according to a synthetic dataset prepared for testing the Splash visual-production workflow. The average share of households with a heat pump rose from 20.5% to 29.8% over the period.

The Netherlands made the largest gain, climbing 18 percentage points from 8% of households in 2021 to 26% in 2025. Poland rose by 12 points and France by 11. Every country in the sample recorded an increase.

Despite that convergence, the geographic divide remains stark. Norway and Sweden ended 2025 at 64% and 62% respectively, while the United Kingdom remained at 9% and Spain at 12%. Finland, at 53%, was the only other country above half of households.

The figures suggest two complementary visual stories: a time-based view of adoption accelerating across the sample, and a 2025 map showing how far the rest of Europe still trails the Nordic leaders.

Methodology and source: Splash Test Desk synthetic dataset, created in July 2026 for product testing. Values are fictional and must not be presented as real-world statistics. The accompanying data.csv is the complete source table.
