## The rule

Water gets a tint. Grey water reads as land the map forgot to fill.

## The evidence

**The Toxmap** draws water *"as a blue-teal tint, never grey"* — its record files this as
`geo-discipline.md`'s rule 7 met in a published piece, which is the rule arriving from the other
direction: written down first, then found in the wild.

**SCMP** sets the Yangtze and its label in the river's own blue, while the country label stays grey.
The hue is doing the same separating job the italic is.

## Why a directed plate is allowed a hue that is not its accent

This base's standing rule is that every colour derives from the direction's own three
(correction 28). Water is the admissible exception, for the same reason a grounded convention is:
**it is not a value.** Nothing on the plate is measured in blue, so a blue cannot be misread as a
quantity — which is exactly the argument that fails for a categorical fill and succeeds here.

The tint is still built against the direction's own ground rather than named as a hex, so it recedes
on a pale page and on a dark one alike, and it is held below the data in contrast by
`the-basemap-gives-up-its-contrast`.
