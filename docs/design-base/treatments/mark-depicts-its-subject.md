## The rule

Where a count is drawn one mark per unit, make the mark depict the unit. A mullet for a mullet, a
human figure for a person. The reader then knows what is being counted before reading a word, and
no legend is needed to say what one mark means.

## Why it is more than a nicety

A unit chart's whole advantage over a bar is that the reader sees the population rather than a
total. A generic dot keeps the population but throws away the recognition; a depicting mark keeps
both. It also makes the piece's own key redundant in the common case — and a key the reader never
has to consult is the cheapest legend there is.

## Where it was seen

ABC's mullet count draws 788 mullet silhouettes, panelled by club. *Four Years of the Russia-Ukraine
War* draws a human figure per thousand people, and states the unit once beside the field.

## What limits it

It costs a drawn asset per subject, which is not a rendering decision this system can take on its
own — a beat has to supply the mark. And it fails where the unit is abstract: there is no drawing of
a megatonne. Where no mark exists, the encoding falls back to a plain unit shape and the treatment
does not apply.
