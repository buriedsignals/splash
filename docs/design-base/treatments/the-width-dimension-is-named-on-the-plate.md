## The rule

Say what the width is. On this form it is the one quantity with no axis and no legend by default.

## Three publications, three answers

Visual Capitalist runs a vertical double-headed arrow down the right edge of its largest cell with
the word `Population` set along it — one annotation, on the one cell that has room for it. The IEA
puts a cumulative axis under the widths, so the second dimension is readable rather than merely
present. Ferdio draws a brace under each column naming its total; that record calls it *"the one
treatment here that a reader could not derive without it, and the one that repairs this form's known
weakness"*.

## Why it is not optional

A marimekko's reader sees a composition — the stacked heights — immediately, and the size of each
column is the thing they are most likely to miss, because nothing on the plate looks like a scale.
An unnamed width is a quantity drawn at full size and left unreadable.

## The negative case, from the same corpus

The IEA's own marginal cost curve is this base's counter-example: a variable-width chart whose
narrowest units fall below a few pixels **has stopped encoding its second dimension**, and colouring
those slivers categorically makes the plate look informative while telling the reader nothing. Naming
the width does not repair that; bucketing the units before drawing does.
