## The rule

Draw the whole scale behind every row. The remainder is a reading; a bare bar cannot give it.

## The two publications

The BBC runs its neutral track the full height of the plot rather than stopping it at the majority
line, which keeps every row comparable and lets a winner **visibly exceed** the target. Datawrapper
ships the same thing by default, and this base's record names what it buys: *"a track that reaches
the plot's ceiling makes the remainder legible, which is the reading a bare bar cannot give."*

## And its second job

The same record calls the flat track *"the honest degradation of a bullet's qualitative bands"*:
where the source data carries no poor/ok/good split, one neutral track keeps the "how much of the way
there" reading the bands would have provided **and manufactures no judgement**. The type page asks
for exactly that; this is what it looks like when a tool ships it as a default.

## The condition

It needs a scale with a stated ceiling — a percentage, a share of a fixed total, a seat count. On an
open scale a full-width track is a claim about a maximum nobody has made.
