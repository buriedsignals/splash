## The rule

Where a beat's claim is that a relationship changes at a level, draw the level and state what
changes.

## Why it needs no published precedent

The fourth treatment found by rendering a beat through the design base and looking at what the plate
did not say, and the sharpest of the four.

This beat's title asserts **beyond roughly $30,000 a person, extra income buys far less extra life
expectancy**. Its plate drew 165 grey dots on a log axis, an x axis, a y axis, and nothing else. Not
the $30,000 line. Not either band. The claim lived entirely in the words, and the graphic beneath it
was a cloud a reader was invited to agree with.

Measured on the beat's own numbers:

| side | countries | life expectancy | spread |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| below $30,000 | 124 | 40.3 – 81.4 | **41.1 years** |
| at or above | 41 | 71.2 – 85.1 | **13.9 years** |

Three times narrower. That is what *far less extra life expectancy* means, stated as a number, and
it was never on the plate.

## What the beat supplies and what the treatment derives

**The break is declared.** A cloud has no natural knee. A renderer that fitted a segmented
regression and drew its own breakpoint would be asserting a model the beat has not stated and the
journalist has not approved — which is exactly the line `crossing-marked`'s declared reference level
and `share-on-the-declared-side-of-a-threshold`'s declared threshold both hold.

**The spreads are arithmetic**, computed from the same pairs the marks were placed from, so the
annotation and the cloud cannot disagree.

## What limits it

**A spread is not a slope.** This states the RANGE of the response on each side, which is what this
beat's copy claims. A beat claiming the slope flattens is claiming something stronger, and a range
does not establish it — two clouds can share a range and have opposite slopes. Where the copy says
slope, the treatment that draws it does not exist yet.

**And the range is sensitive to one point.** Seychelles at 71.2 years sets the low end of the upper
band single-handed; a render audit of this beat once caught the brief and the plate both saying "76
to 85" for that reason. The number is honest and it is fragile, which is a fact about ranges and is
worth the beat knowing.
