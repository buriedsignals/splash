## The rule

Tint the area between the curve and the level it is measured against: the accent's own tint where
the series is above, a neutral where it is below.

## Why it needs no published precedent

Both quantities are the beat's own — its series and its declared reference. The band is their
difference, drawn. Nothing is imported.

## What it does

**It makes the claim visible instead of asserted.** A title that says "less than in 1967" asks the
reader to compare a line against a dashed rule by eye. The band gives that comparison a surface, and
the excess over seventy years becomes a quantity rather than an inference.

**One accent, two weights.** Above the level carries the accent's tint; below carries a neutral. The
two sides differ in WEIGHT, not in hue, so `doctrine/references/visual-system.md`'s single-accent
rule holds — a second hue there would say the two sides were different things rather than two signs
of one.

## What limits it

Measured on this beat: the neutral band under the level for 1950–1967 is true but draws the eye to
the pre-history rather than to the subject. Where the series spends a long stretch on the
uninteresting side, the treatment should tint only from the peak forward, and that variant is not
yet filed.
