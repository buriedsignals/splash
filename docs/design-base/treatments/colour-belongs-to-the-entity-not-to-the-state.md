## The rule

On a slope the entity travels and the states are fixed positions. Give the hue to the thing that
travels.

## The evidence, and the corpus names the fork itself

`100.datavizproject.com`'s viz17: *"colour belongs to the entity, not to the date. Both ends of a
line share one hue. Compare #19, where the same desk gives colour to the two dates instead — **the
family's one real fork**."*

Information is Beautiful labels each rail *"in the service's colour"*, so the hue tracks the entity
across both rails and the label is the legend — the treatment already filed as
`direct-end-label-in-the-series-colour`, met on a slope.

## The other answer, and when it is right

Reuters draws its paired form as **offset tint pairs**, one hue at two lightnesses, because there the
two STATES are the subject and the entities are the rows. That is
`two-states-of-one-measure-are-one-hue-at-two-chromas`.

The condition is which thing the reader has to follow. A plate does not get both: hue on the entity
and hue on the state at once is two encodings in one channel.
