# context-in-neutral-at-the-subject-scale

- kind: imported
- name: The comparison set shares the subject's unit and is drawn in neutral
- applies: the beat compares its subject against other entities, other periods, or historical
  precedents that are not themselves the subject
- draws: axis, annot
- priority: 5
- evidence: informationisbeautiful-net-visualizations-four-years-of-the-russia-ukr
- evidence: 100-datavizproject-com-data-type-viz57
- detect: every comparison mark is drawn at the same scale as the subject's marks, and its fill is
  a step from the ground toward the ink rather than a hue

## The rule

When a beat sets its subject against context — past conflicts, an earlier year, peer countries —
draw the context **at the subject's own unit and scale**, and in neutral. Two things follow, and
both matter: the comparison becomes countable in the same object, and the colour alone says which
one the story is about.

## Why it is more than a nicety

A comparison drawn on its own scale, in its own chart, is not a comparison — it is two pictures the
reader is asked to reconcile. And a comparison drawn in a second hue creates a second accent, which
`doctrine/references/visual-system.md` refuses precisely because the reader then cannot tell which
series the story is about.

## Where it was seen

*Four Years of the Russia-Ukraine War* (Information is Beautiful) draws Chechnya, Afghanistan,
Korea, Vietnam and the US in the Second World War as the **same human pictogram at the same one
figure per thousand**, in grey, beside the subject in colour. `viz57` (Ferdio) draws the 2004 value
as a grey arc concentric with the accented 2022 one — same geometry, same scale, neutral past.

## What limits it

It needs a shared unit to exist. Where the subject and its context are measured in different things,
this treatment does not apply and the comparison has to be made in prose.
