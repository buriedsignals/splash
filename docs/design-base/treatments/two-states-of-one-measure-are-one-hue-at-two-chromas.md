## The rule

One hue, two chromas. Two hues would say two categories.

## The two publications

Datawrapper draws value and target as one hue at two chromas — this base's record: *"the reader is
told these are two states of the same quantity before reading a single label. Costs nothing, and it
is the only colour decision the chart makes."* Statista reaches the same rule from the other side:
*"the tint of the same hue, not a second hue, for the earlier state. Two states of one measure should
not read as two categories."*

## Where it meets this base's own palette rule

`METHOD.md` correction 28 arrives here from the corpus rather than from a repair: the two chromas are
steps of the DIRECTION's own accent, and no second hue is imported. A bullet is the smallest case of
that rule — two marks, one measure, one hue.

## The geometry that goes with it

Datawrapper's record adds the arrangement that makes two lengths legible on one row without a tick:
**the thin saturated bar in front of the thick pale one.** The pale bar reads as the backdrop the
type page asks for and the saturated one as the measure, from area and chroma alone.

## Corroborated in a second family — 2026-09-09

The `paired` harvest reached the same rule from three more publications, on a different form: ABC
draws "a two-measure pair as one hue at two lightnesses, adjacent, from a shared origin"; Reuters
draws "the pair as two tints of one hue and lets the earlier one fall into the furniture";
`100.datavizproject.com`'s viz3 draws "the earlier state as a lighter tint of the later state's own
hue — it keeps the pair to one colour identity and still says which one the story is about".

**Five publications across two families now say it.** That is the widest agreement in this base, and
it is the rule `METHOD.md` correction 28 arrived at from the other direction, by drawing a plate
badly twice.
