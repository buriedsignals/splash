## The rule

A control that only redraws marks makes the reader do the reading twice. A control that rewrites the
sentence hands back an answer.

## The evidence — three publications, three families

**The Guardian**, on a choropleth: *"the reader's own number is the parameter. The headline claim is
stated against it in words — '91% of England and Wales would be beyond your means in 2014' — so the
map answers a question the reader asked rather than one the newsroom chose."*

**didoesdigital**, on a polar matrix: *"the reader's own selection is the parameter"* —
`14 out of 20 favourite foods are selected`.

**ProPublica**, Miseducation: *"the sentence is generated by the controls."* Change `RACE` to
`Hispanic` or `MEASURE` to something else and the prose beside the chart changes with it.

## What it costs, and why it is worth it

The sentence has to be composed from the same numbers the marks are drawn from, or the two will drift
apart — which is the same discipline `claims-grounded-in-data` already enforces on a static plate,
carried into a state the newsroom does not see before publishing. A generated sentence is a claim, so
it is asserted like one.
