## The rule

Direction from the picture, level from the numbers, magnitude from the delta — three facts, three
places, three weights, none of them competing.

## The evidence

ABC: *"the change is stated three times, in three registers, each in its own place — as the slope's
angle; as the two values printed at the two ends; and as a **percent-change pill** at the right,
filled with the series' own colour."* And, plainly: *"the pill separates the delta from the value.
The end block reads: series name in caps and bold, then the value, then the pill. Three different
facts, three different weights, one column."*

`100.datavizproject.com`'s viz54 prints the same third fact as a percentage beside each line, and its
record checks the arithmetic against the archive's own sibling figure: 4→10 is +150 %, 13→15 is
+15.4 %, 5→8 is +60 %.

## Why it is filed separately from the derived-number rule

`the-verdict-is-written-as-a-derived-number` already says a derived figure is written rather than
left to be inferred. What this adds is that the derived figure **may not share a register with the
levels it was derived from** — otherwise a reader meets three facts as one column of numbers and has
to work out which is which.

## The condition

Only where the two rails are the same measure. A slope between revenue-per-play and audience has no
delta to write: the difference between two different units is not a number.
