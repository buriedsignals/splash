# the-one-interaction-sits-on-the-plate

- kind: imported
- name: The control and its invitation sit with the graphic, not in prose above or below it
- applies: the beat puts one or more controls on the page
- draws: annot, axis
- priority: 7
- evidence: projects-propublica-org-toxmap
- evidence: yemen-topaz-vercel-app
- detect: every control is inside the graphic's own frame on the delivered page

## The rule

Put the control where the reader is looking. A control described in prose above the graphic is a
control most readers never find.

## The evidence

**ProPublica**, Toxmap: *"a pill invites the one interaction that matters — 'Click inside a hot spot
to see how risks combine there' — set ON THE PLATE, in its own dark chip, rather than in prose above
the map."*

**Buried Signals**, Yemen: *"a single year filter along the bottom edge, set as a row of years with
`ALL` selected — the piece's one interaction, placed on the plate rather than above it."*

ProPublica says it again on an unrelated piece — *"the instruction sits with the graphic, in
italic"* — which is corroboration, not a second publication.

## The word "one"

Both records say **the** interaction, singular, and both pieces have one. A page with five controls
has not made the reader more powerful; it has made them responsible for finding the view the
newsroom should have chosen. Where a beat needs more than one, each still sits on the plate, and
`the-state-you-are-in-is-louder-than-the-controls` becomes the load-bearing rule.
