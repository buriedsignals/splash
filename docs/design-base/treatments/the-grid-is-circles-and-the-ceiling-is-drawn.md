## The rule

The grid is circles. The ceiling is drawn. The only polygon on the plate belongs to the data.

## The measurement under it

StatsBomb draws five or six full circles at even radii and no polygonal gridlines at all — the
record's own words: *"the only polygon on the plate is the player's"*. The Analyst draws a light
full circle at the outside meaning the 100th percentile, with dashed rings inside it, so a wedge is
read against a visible ceiling rather than against nothing.

## Why both halves are one rule

A polygonal web is a shape, and a radar's whole read is a shape: the grid ends up competing with the
data it is supposed to support, and at low values the two are hard to tell apart. And a radius with
no drawn ceiling is a length against nothing — the reader cannot tell 60 % of the way out from 60 %
of anything.

## What limits it

A ring needs a number on it or the ceiling is still unstated; `references/types/radar.md` asks for
"at least a faint gridline and one labelled ring". This treatment draws the rings; it is
`a-radius-is-not-read-by-eye` that puts the quantities on the plate.
