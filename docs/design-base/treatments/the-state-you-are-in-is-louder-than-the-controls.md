## The rule

A reader must never be looking at a filtered subset and believe it is the whole.

## The evidence

**NPR**: *"'Showing all books' is set at `Gotham SSm | 24 | 500` against the 26 category labels at
`Gotham SSm | 15 | 400`. The filter you have APPLIED is displayed larger than the filters you might
apply."*

**Information is Beautiful**: *"the legend is the filter, and the piece says so"* — a tracked-caps
line above the legend saying as much, so the key and the control are one object and the reader is
told which it is.

## Why this belongs in this base rather than in a component

It is the interaction-era form of a rule the base already holds for static plates: **a plate states
its own limits.** A static beat prints "40 European countries, 2024" in its standfirst; an
interactive one has a standfirst that changes, and the same obligation. A filtered view that does not
say it is filtered is a plate lying about its own extent — and it is a lie the newsroom never sees,
because the newsroom looks at the default.
