## The rule

The boxes, bands and whiskers are furniture. Colour is for the thing being argued.

## The measurement under it

Nature's `nmeth.2813` figure 1 is drawn without a single chromatic pixel — this base's own record
reads `pixel.chromatic` empty, `pixel.shape` monochrome, `pixel.ramped` 0. Its box is the plate's
largest non-white neutral, `#D4D4D5` at 3.253 %, and every rule, letter and dot outline together
come to under 1.2 % of the frame. NSIDC's Charctic reaches the same arrangement from the other side:
median and both nested bands in greys, and the one or two years under discussion drawn in colour
over them.

The strictest reading in this corpus puts the argued colour at **0.112 % of a frame**. That is the
posture: a distribution plate can hold five statistics per group and still have its whole accent
budget unspent.

## What it is NOT

It is not "no colour". A beat whose subject IS one of the groups spends the accent on that group,
and the rule is what makes that legible: on a plate where nothing else carries chroma, one coloured
box is an argument rather than a decoration.

## What limits it

A beat comparing two groups deliberately may spend two hues — `references/types/boxplot.md` allows
exactly two, and only for that reason. Past that the summaries are competing with each other and
the accent no longer marks anything.
