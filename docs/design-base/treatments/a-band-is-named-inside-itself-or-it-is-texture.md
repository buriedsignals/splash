## The rule

Name the band inside the band. On this form the label is not decoration — it is the whole naming
mechanism.

## Four publications, one of them negative

Ferdio: *"label the band inside the band, at its widest interior point, in ink measured against that
fill — because the form has no axis, this is not decoration."* UNHCR: *"scale the in-band label with
the band. Big band, big name; small band, small name; tiny band, nothing. It shows the long tail
instead of pretending it away."* The New York Times labels a few bands at their own peak with a
leader dot **and accepts that the rest are texture**.

And Lee Byron's own paper figure is the counter-example this base learned most from: strip the
labels and *"nothing on this plate can be turned back into a number"*. The form that a designer
invented, drawn by the designer who invented it, is unreadable without the thing this rule asks for.

## What "scaled to the band" costs

Bands that cannot hold their name get nothing — which is honest, and is why the rule reads "every
band a reader COULD name". The alternative, a leader out to a gutter for each of twenty bands, is a
second chart.
