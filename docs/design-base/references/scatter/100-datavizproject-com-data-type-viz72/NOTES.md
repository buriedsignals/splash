# 100 datavizproject — #72, absolute change against relative change

- url: https://100.datavizproject.com/data-type/viz72/
- archive: datavizproject
- type: scatter — two quantitative axes, both **derived** from the same underlying variable
- export: static
- readAs: the encoding as the page draws it, at rest

## What it is

The same three countries on **x = absolute change** (0 → 8, counts) against **y = percentual change**
(0 % → 200 %). Neither axis is a raw reading: both are computed from the same two numbers the whole
archive is built on.

## What it does with information

**Plotting the two ways of measuring one change against each other is the encoding.** Denmark grew
by 6 sites and by 150 %; Sweden grew by 2 and by 15 %; Norway by 3 and by 60 %. On a bar chart you
must pick one of those and the other disappears. Here the disagreement between them *is* the
picture: Denmark is far right *and* far up, Sweden is neither, and Norway's position says that
"middling in absolute terms" and "middling in relative terms" are not the same middling.

This is the one thing in this family a beat can do **without borrowing anything**: the two derived
quantities are already in the beat's own data the moment it has a before and an after.

**The label sits beside the dot, not above it, and is in ink rather than in the dot's colour.**
`Denmark`, `Norway`, `Sweden` in near-black bold, one word-space right of a small coloured dot, on
the dot's own baseline. The dot keeps the colour; the word keeps the contrast. Compare `#7` in this
same archive, which puts the name in a coloured pill instead — the same desk, two different answers,
which is a useful reminder that neither is a house rule.

**The axis titles name the derivation, not the variable.** `Absolute change` and `Percentual
change`, in grey mixed case, y rotated. A reader knows immediately that neither axis is a level.

**The units are on the ticks and they differ per axis** — bare counts on x, `%` on y — so the two
scales cannot be confused for one.

## What it does with style

Same site-chrome contamination as the other records from this archive. Re-measured on the chart card
(`crop 308,170,824,730`): ground **`#FFFFFF` at 97.5 %**; chromatic `#F05440` (7°) and `#3274DA`
(216°) each at ~0.01 % — three dots on a mostly empty plate — with the third entity's `#283250`
again falling on the neutral side of the chroma split. A palette of three small marks is at the very
bottom of what the pixel route can say anything about; the hues are identifiable, the coverages are
noise.

**And the contamination here is not merely dilution.** The site header's `#3274DA` is **the same
hue as the chart's own blue entity** (`#3274D8`, 216° in both). On the uncropped page shot a 216°
cluster cannot be attributed to the chart at all. Every colour figure above is the cropped
re-measurement; nothing is quoted from this record's own `pixel` field.

## What is transferable

- **Scatter the absolute change against the relative change** whenever a beat has a before and an
  after for several entities. It needs no published precedent to justify: the beat's own data
  already holds both numbers, and drawing them against each other is the only way to show that they
  rank the entities differently.
- **Label beside the mark in ink, with the mark keeping the colour**, where a coloured word would
  lose contrast.
- **Name the derivation in the axis title**, so a reader is never left to assume a level.
