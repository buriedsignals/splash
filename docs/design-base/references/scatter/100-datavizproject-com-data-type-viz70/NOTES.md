# 100 datavizproject — #70, the connected scatter

- url: https://100.datavizproject.com/data-type/viz70/
- archive: datavizproject
- type: connected scatter — two quantitative axes, two states per entity joined by a path
- export: static
- readAs: the encoding as the page draws it, at rest

## What it is

The three countries on **x = share of Scandinavian sites** (0 → 60 %) against **y = number of
sites** (0 → 15). Each country appears **twice** — once for each date — and its two points are
joined.

## What it does with information

**The earlier state is a hollow ring, the later state a filled disc, in the same colour.** No key
says so. It works because the convention is old and a reader carries it: an outline is what a thing
*was*, a solid is what it *is*. The same logic
`palette/references/subject-conventions.md` asks for — a convention the reader already holds is the
only kind worth spending on.

**The two states are joined by a dotted arc, and only the later one is labelled.** `SE`, `DK`, `NO`
sit beside the filled disc, in the entity's colour; the hollow ring carries nothing. The label
belongs to the entity, and the entity's current position is where a reader will look for it.

**A count on one axis and a share on the other.** `Number of sites` against `Share of Scandinavian
sites`: the same underlying quantity as a level and as a proportion of the group. Sweden's arc runs
down-right — it gained sites *and* lost share, because the others gained faster. That reading exists
in no single-axis chart of this data.

**The arc is curved, not straight.** A straight segment between two points would read as
interpolation — as if there were intermediate states on the line. A curve that plainly is not the
shortest path reads as a link rather than a trajectory. (This is a reading of the drawing's intent,
not something the piece states.)

## What it does with style

Same site-chrome contamination as the other records from this archive. Re-measured on the chart card
(`crop 308,170,824,730`): ground **`#FFFFFF` at 97.7 %**; chromatic `#EE5440` (7°) and `#3274D8`
(216°) at 0.03 % each; furniture `#F4F6F6` / `#EEF1F2` / `#EBEFF0` at 0.2–0.5 % — three steps of
near-white for the gridlines. Almost the entire plate is paper, which is what a six-point chart
looks like when it is not padded.

**And the contamination here is not merely dilution.** The site header's `#3274DA` is **the same
hue as the chart's own blue entity** (`#3274D8`, 216° in both). On the uncropped page shot a 216°
cluster cannot be attributed to the chart at all. Every colour figure above is the cropped
re-measurement; nothing is quoted from this record's own `pixel` field.

## What is transferable

- **Hollow ring for before, filled disc for after, same hue, joined by a curved dotted link**, and
  no key for it.
- **Label the later state only.**
- **Put the level on one axis and the share of the group on the other** when "grew but shrank
  relatively" is a thing that could be true.

## What is this piece's own

The triad and the house sans.
