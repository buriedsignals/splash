## What it is

Every recognised dog breed placed on **x = "our data score"** (a composite the poster builds in
front of the reader) against **y = "popularity"**, drawn as a silhouette of that breed.

## What it does with information

**The axes cross inside the plot, at the origin, with an arrowhead on each.** Not an L-frame in the
corner: a horizontal rule with `our data score` written on it at the left, a vertical rule with
`popularity` written on it at the top, both arrow-tipped, and the four quadrants that fall out of
the crossing. A reader reads "above the line / right of the line" before reading any number.

**The quadrants are named, in words, at their outer corners.** `Inexplicably Overrated` top-left and
`Hot Dogs!` top-right, set large, light and loose in the poster's own hand. The other two sit below
the fold of a 1440 × 900 capture and were not read. Naming the quadrant is the piece's whole
argument; the axes only measure it.

**The composite index is decomposed at the axis it belongs to.** Beside the x axis's own name sits a
two-row grid of six labelled icons — `intelligence`, `costs`, `longevity`, `grooming`, `ailments`,
`appetite` — which is what "our data score" is made of. The definition sits where the reader meets
the scale, not in a methodology note.

**The mark depicts its subject and encodes two more channels at once.** Each breed is its own
silhouette; the silhouette's **size** carries the breed's size and its **colour** carries its
kennel group. Three separate keys run in a row across the top-right, above the plot and level with
the title: `INTELLIGENCE` (two specimen dogs, `dumb` / `clever`), `SIZE` (three specimen dogs,
`sml med` / `lrge`), and seven group names each set **in its own colour with no swatch beside it**
(`Herding`, `Hound`, `Non-sporting`, `Sporting`, `Terrier`, `Toy`, `Working`). The key for the
colour channel *is* coloured words.

**Every mark is named, and the name is set in the mark's own colour** at the mark's foot.

## What is transferable

- **Cross the axes at the origin and name the quadrants in words.** A scatter whose story is
  "which corner is this in" should say which corner is which, rather than making the reader
  reconstruct it from two scales.
- **Decompose a composite index next to the axis that carries it.**
- **Set a categorical key as coloured words with no swatches**, when the marks are themselves that
  colour.
- **A depicting mark can carry size and colour as well as position** — `mark-depicts-its-subject`
  is filed on unit charts; here it is doing four channels at once on a scatter.
- **Split a two-clause title by weight on one line.**

## What is this piece's own

The cream ground, the slate-green ink, the drawn breed silhouettes, and the poster's hand-set feel.
The breed drawings are a per-subject asset, which is the standing limit on `mark-depicts-its-subject`.
