## What it is

Roughly sixty films, one bubble each, on a single horizontal axis running **16 % → 28 % → 40 %** and
a vertical axis of release year (2007 at the foot, 2017 at the head). The axis is the
`% gap between audience & critics 'rotten tomatoes' score` — a *derived* number: the pair of scores
has been subtracted and only the difference is drawn. Bubble area is the film's budget; bubble hue is
categorical and unexplained in the frame; one film, *The Last Jedi*, is drawn as an outlined ring
with a leader to a four-line note.

## What it does with information

**Neither state is drawn. The difference is.** This is the family's third answer to "how is the
change carried" — not by two marks, not by a connector, but by one mark standing for the gap between
them. It buys precision on the comparison and gives up the levels entirely: nothing here says whether
critics loved a film or merely liked it.

**Both terms of the vanished pair are named in the display line**, and in two inks: *Movies Critics
Loved,* in white and *But Audiences Really Didn't* in grey. The headline reconstructs the pair the
encoding threw away. The deck then states the derived measure in full. ProPublica's *Miseducation*
does the same on a map, independently: **when the pair is collapsed, both terms are named in the
sentence that sits with the graphic.**

**The axis end is labelled with what it MEANS, not with what it measures.** The right end carries
`AUDIENCE REALLY HATES` beside the `40%` tick. The scale gets a value and a meaning at the same
place.

**The subject is picked out with a ring and a leader, not with a colour.** *The Last Jedi* keeps
whatever hue its category gives it and is emphasised by an outline plus a note — so the emphasis is
additive and does not cost the encoding a channel. Reuters' battleground states are ringed the same
way in the same family.

## What it does with style

A dark ground — the pixel route reports `#333333` at **76.3 %** of the page shot. Saturated
categorical bubble hues (yellow, cyan, green, magenta, orange, grey) on the dark, with white and grey
type.

**The record's chromatic palette is the SITE'S PROMOTIONAL BANNER, not this graphic.** All three
dark-ground Information is Beautiful records harvested for this family report the *identical* four
chromatic colours — `#D4537A` 0.70 %, `#EAAB4A` 0.68 %, `#ECB445` 0.65 %, `#E69B53` 0.63 %, clusters
at 342° and 37° — for three completely different pictures. That is the pink→amber "New! Learn to do
data-viz" bar across the top of every page. These posters are rasters with no `<svg>`, `<canvas>` or
`<figure><img>` large enough for `largestGraphic`, so the pixel route fell back to the page
screenshot and the banner was the only chromatic thing in it. A close cousin of `METHOD.md`'s
correction 3, and it must not be read as a measurement of the graphic.

Style route: **two families**, **zero italic runs**, 8 tracked and 8 case-transformed runs, all site
chrome, and **zero mark colours** — the poster is a raster.

## What is transferable

- **Collapsing a pair to its difference is a real option, and it costs the levels.** Take it when the
  gap is the story and the levels are not.
- **Name both terms of the collapsed pair in the display line**, and let the ink say which one the
  piece is about.
- **Label an axis end with its meaning as well as its value.**
- **Emphasise one mark with an outline and a note rather than by recolouring it**, so it keeps its
  category.

## What is this piece's own

The dark ground and the saturated bubble set; the film subject; the hand-set label placement.

## What was not verified

What the bubble hues encode — no key is visible in the captured frame, so the categorical channel
could not be read. Whether bubble area or diameter carries budget. Whether the axis is truncated at
16 % (it does not begin at 0) — it plainly is, and the effect on the reading was not assessed. The
Rotten Tomatoes figures themselves.
