## What it is

Two neighbourhoods, **GARDENA** and **FREMONT**, set against each other. A single `0%` at the top of
the frame; from it, arcs sweep left for one place and right for the other, four per side, on a shared
radial scale ticked `50%` on both sides. The four measures — share of Black kids in the
neighbourhood, share who stayed as adults, median household income in 1990, median household income
in 2016 — are drawn in the same four hues on both sides. Each side's readings are listed in a column
at its own outer edge.

## What it does with information

**The comparison is made by mirroring one scale, not by placing two charts side by side.** There is
one zero, one radius, one tick set; the two entities grow away from each other out of it. Which side
reaches further IS the comparison, and no axis has to be read twice.

**Each side's type is aligned to its own outer edge**, so both label blocks are ragged toward the
middle and the two of them frame the picture rather than crowding it. Information is Beautiful's
*Deniers vs The Consensus* does exactly this in a prose layout, independently — two publications, two
media, one rule.

**One key, used identically on both sides.** Blue, green, crimson, amber mean the same four measures
left and right, so the reader compares measure to measure rather than side to side.

**Two of the four hues are themselves a paired comparison** — crimson for 1990 income and amber for
2016 — nested inside the left/right pair. Two pairings on one plate, distinguished by being on
different channels: one is position, the other is hue.

**Each reading is printed with its own value and its own sentence**, e.g. `$81K (84TH PERCENTILE)` /
`MEDIAN HOUSEHOLD INCOME IN 1990`. The percentile in parentheses gives the absolute figure a national
frame — a second reading of the same number, next to it.

**A value too small to draw is written rather than dropped**: Fremont's second measure reads
`<5%`, and its arc is a stub.

## What it does with style

Measured on `graphic-scrolled.png`: ground `#FEF9F1` at **68.2 %** with a second near-identical paper
`#FBF6EE` at 19.5 % (about 88 % paper in total); the palette reads **categorical, three hue clusters**
at 347° (`#D8284F`, 2.4 %), 39° (`#EFAE38`, 2.0 %) and 225° (`#5367A2`, 0.5 %), with the fourth
series — a muted green `#82A884` at 0.6 % — falling into the *neutral* set because its chroma is too
low to register as a pole. A four-series key of which the route can only see three.

The record's own `graphic.png` measures the illustrated title card instead: ground `#FEF9F1` at
68.9 %, clusters at 350° and 227°, and `stroke rgb(38, 38, 38)` ×1445 — the hand-drawn US outline.

Style route, four families and a very clear assignment: **Tiempos Text Web** 18 / 400 for quoted
speech (26 runs), **National 2 Web** 18 / 400 for the writer's own prose (26 runs) — *two body
faces, one per voice* — and **DuBois Regular** for every label, always **uppercase**: 10 / 700 for
place names, 14 / 700 and 14 / 400 for credits, 18 / 700 for emphasis. **Zero italic runs, zero
tracked runs, 79 case-transformed runs.** The hierarchy is family, weight and case; tracking is never
touched. Text column 576 px / **64 ch**.

## What is transferable

- **Mirror one scale about a shared centre for a two-entity comparison**, and align each side's type
  to its own outer edge.
- **Use one key on both sides** so the reader compares measure to measure.
- **Nest a second pairing on a different channel** — position for the entities, hue for the dates —
  rather than splitting the plate again.
- **Give a figure its own frame in parentheses** (`84TH PERCENTILE`) beside the raw value.
- **Write a value that is too small to draw** rather than letting the reader see nothing.
- **Two body faces, one per voice** — a serif for the people quoted, a sans for the reporter — is a
  distinction the reader learns in one paragraph and keeps.

## What is this piece's own

The paper `#FEF9F1` and the coffee-ring texture; DuBois Regular, named for W. E. B. Du Bois and
chosen for this subject; the radial geometry, which makes lengths harder to compare than a mirrored
bar pair would.

## What was not verified

Whether the four arcs on a side share one radial scale — `0%` and `50%` are ticked, and the two
income arcs are dollars rather than percentages, so at least two different scales are certainly in
play on one shared radius and the note could not establish how they are reconciled. The 132,003
figure quoted elsewhere on the page. Whether other neighbourhood pairs are shown further on: only
this one was read.
