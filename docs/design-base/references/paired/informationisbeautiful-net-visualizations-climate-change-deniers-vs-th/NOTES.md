- archive: informationisbeautiful

## What it is

A poster laid out as a debate. A rule across the top carries `InformationIsBeautiful.net` on the
left and `X vs Y No 2` on the right. Beneath it, two headings —
**THE GLOBAL WARMING SKEPTICS** in pink on the left, **THE SCIENTIFIC CONSENSUS** in green on the
right — with **vs** in white between them. Each side then states its case in its own colour, claim by
claim; between the two claim blocks, in the middle of the page, sits the disputed evidence itself:
an Arctic temperature series in red, a CO₂ series in blue, and stacked fossil-fuel areas in greys.

## What it does with information

**The pair is the LAYOUT, and the shared centre is the argument.** Each side owns a column, and the
whole page is symmetric about the gutter. Nothing in the composition privileges either side; the
positions do the comparing.

**Each side's type is aligned to its own outer edge**, so both blocks are ragged toward the middle
and the two columns frame the centre rather than crowding it. The left column is left-aligned, the
right is right-aligned. The same move appears in The Pudding's *upward mobility* radial —
independently, in a different medium — and it is what makes a mirrored layout read as one figure
instead of two.

**The shared evidence is drawn once, in the gutter, in NEITHER side's colour.** The temperature and
CO₂ chart belongs to the argument, not to a party; a reader can look at it without being told in
advance what to conclude. That is the strongest thing on the page.

**Claims are paired row by row.** Each pink assertion is set opposite the green answer to it, on a
shared baseline, divided by a dashed hairline. The reader compares like with like because the layout
made them adjacent.

**The two sides are named at full display size before either argues.** The reader knows who is
speaking before they read a word of it.

## What it does with style

A near-black ground — the pixel route reports `#231F20` at **62.9 %** of the page shot, with
`#F6F6F6` at 8.7 % and `#FAFAFA` at 6.1 % (the site's own light chrome above and below the
poster). Two saturated poles read by eye as a pink and a green, held to the two sides and used
nowhere else; the middle chart uses a third set (red, blue, greys) that belongs to neither.

**The record's chromatic palette is the SITE'S PROMOTIONAL BANNER, not this graphic.** All three
dark-ground Information is Beautiful records harvested for this family report the *identical* four
chromatic colours — `#D4537A` 0.70 %, `#EAAB4A` 0.68 %, `#ECB445` 0.65 %, `#E69B53` 0.63 %, clusters
at 342° and 37° — for three completely different pictures. That is the pink→amber "New! Learn to do
data-viz" bar across the top of every page. These posters are rasters with no `<svg>`, `<canvas>` or
`<figure><img>` large enough for `largestGraphic`, so the pixel route fell back to the page
screenshot and the banner was the only chromatic thing in it. A close cousin of `METHOD.md`'s
correction 3, and it must not be read as a measurement of the graphic.

Style route: **two families** (IBM Plex Sans, Quicksand), **zero italic runs**, 11 tracked runs and 8
case-transformed runs — all of them site chrome, and **zero mark colours**. The poster's own type
(a bold humanist sans, all-caps headings) was read by eye and not measured.

## What is transferable

- **When a beat is a two-sided comparison, make the page symmetric about a shared centre**, and align
  each side's type to its own outer edge so the two blocks frame the gutter.
- **Give each side one colour, used for nothing else**, and name both sides at display size before
  either speaks.
- **Put the shared evidence in the gutter, in neither side's colour.** A comparison whose evidence
  wears one side's accent has already decided.
- **Pair the claims row by row on a shared baseline** so the reader compares like with like without
  scrolling back.
