## What it is

A twenty-nine-row overlaid bar chart of NATO members' military spending in 2014 and 2025, whose
**title states a target — "All NATO Members To Reach 2-Percent Goal in 2025" — and whose target is
then never drawn at all.** Each country's row carries a pale 2014 bar with a dark 2025 bar continuing
from where it ends, on a shared 0–80bn axis; to the right of the plot, outside the axis, a column
headed **"Share of real GDP (in %)"** gives each country's 2025 percentage in green: 2.4, 2.0, 2.1,
2.0, 2.0, 4.5, …

The target — 2 % — appears in the headline, in the column header's unit, and in every value in that
column being ≥ 2.0. It appears nowhere as a mark.

What was read: Statista's own published "Chart of the Day" infographic image, inside the article,
with its CC licence marks and wordmark on the plate. Not a promo card, not a hero.

## What it does with information

- **The measure and the target verdict are in different units, so they are in different columns.**
  The bars are billions of constant 2021 dollars; the target is a share of GDP. Rather than
  normalise, the chart puts the bars on the axis and the target-relevant number in a table column
  beside them. This is the honest solution to the type page's warning that "two KPIs measured in
  different units have nothing in common to be forced onto one axis" — and the price is that the
  verdict is read twenty-nine times, one row at a time, instead of once.
- **The green ink is the entire target apparatus.** `#5E9205` occupies **0.184 % of the frame** — a
  fifth of a percent — and carries the whole editorial claim of the headline.
- **Nothing marks who is closest to failing.** Because the column is numbers and not a mark, a reader
  cannot see at a glance that Lithuania at 4.0 and Bulgaria at 2.1 are far apart; both are just
  black-on-white digits in green. A bullet's ticks would have shown it.
- **The bar's own encoding is not the target at all** — it is 2014 versus 2025, a change over time.
  Two different arguments share one row: growth (the bar) and compliance (the column).
- **Sorting is by 2025 magnitude, not by distance from target.** The reader's likeliest question,
  "who is behind", is the one the sort does not answer.
- **A call-out box floats inside the plot's empty upper-right**, carrying the two-column summary
  `United States $845.3B` vs `All other NATO members $545.2B` and `3.2 % of real GDP`, on a white
  card with a shadow — the chart's own aggregate stated as a second, smaller chart in the space the
  long tail of small countries leaves free.
- Flags stand in for a second identifier beside every country name, and the NATO roundel sits as a
  watermark disc in the same empty space.

## What is transferable

- **Spend the plate's only foreign hue on the target verdict.** A single-hue chart with one green
  column is a design in which the reader's eye cannot avoid the number the headline is about.
- **When measure and target are in different units, don't force them onto one axis** — put the
  target-relevant figure in a labelled column beside the plot and name its unit in the column
  header. This is the substitution a desk actually reaches for, and the type page should say so.
- **Fill the plot's dead space with the aggregate.** A long-tailed ranking leaves a large empty
  wedge; a small summary card there is worth more than tightening the axis.
- **The tint of the same hue, not a second hue, for the earlier state.** Two states of one measure
  should not read as two categories.

## What was not verified

- **No type from the graphic.** It is a raster, `style.typeSource` says so, and the plate's own
  families, sizes and weights are unmeasured.
- The bars' construction was read by eye and is ambiguous at this resolution: the pale 2014 bar and
  the dark 2025 bar appear **contiguous** (dark starting where pale ends), which would make the dark
  segment the *increase* rather than the 2025 total. The legend says `2014` / `2025`, which implies
  overlay. Which of the two it is could not be settled from the picture, and no value labels exist to
  settle it. **Every reading above about the bar encoding is provisional on this.**
- `#5E9205` was identified as the target column by position and by being the only non-blue hue in
  the frame; it was not sampled from a named pixel.
- Statista is a data vendor with an editorial "Chart of the Day" desk. It is counted here as one
  publication, and this family's corpus contains a **second Statista record** which corroborates
  nothing (`statista-com-chart-35058-nato-2-timeline`).
- The plate carries a Creative Commons mark; the licence terms were not read.
