# Our World in Data — *The Demography of the World Population from 1950 to 2100*

`https://ourworldindata.org/global-population-pyramid` · harvested 2026-09-08 · archive `search`.

## What it is

**The form pushed to nine states on one plate.** The record's graphic is an `img` 845 × 586 at
`documentTop` 1637, `nearTheTop: true`, `routes.pixel.measuredFrom: "graphic.png"`, and it is OWID's
own plate, titled *The Demography of the World Population from 1950 to 2100*, subtitled *Shown is the
age distribution of the world population – by sex – from 1950 to 2018 and the UN Population
Division's projection until 2100.* A consent dialog was dismissed
(`consent: button "Accept optional cookies"`).

It is a population pyramid by every structural test — one ordered dimension (age) running up the
centre, one population split exactly two ways, both sides growing outward from a shared zero on one
mirrored magnitude scale — **and it is not drawn as bars.** Each of nine dated populations is a
filled silhouette nested inside the next, so what would be a hundred bands becomes a continuous
envelope.

**This page is also filed under `histogram`, deliberately.** There it is read for what it says about
when a histogram should stop binning; here it is read as the family's answer to *"what happens when
you need more than two states on one pyramid"*. **It is one publication and it counts once.** The
record is an independent re-harvest, not a copy.

## What it does with information

- **Mirrored about a shared zero**, `Men` left and `Women` right, named **in words at the foot of
  each half at display size**. No legend — the third publication in this family to name the halves
  rather than key them.
- **The magnitude axis is drawn once, along the top, and counts outward from the centre**:
  `70 Million 60 … 10 Million | 10 Million … 60 70 Million`. Both halves on one scale, both sides
  positive, **and the unit is written into the tick**, not banished to an axis title.
- **The age axis is on the left in the variable's own unit** — `10 years`, `20 years`, … `90 years`.
  Bin edges, not bin index. Like PopulationPyramid.net and unlike ONS and StatCan, the ordered
  dimension is in the margin rather than a centre gutter — here of necessity, because the centre is
  where nine envelopes converge.
- **Every silhouette is labelled in place, on the shape itself**, with its year set along the slope
  of the curve: `1950 1960 1970 1980 1990 2018 2050 2075 2100`, **on both halves**. Nine series,
  eighteen direct labels, no key.
- **A ranked reading is set out in the right margin as a list**: median age at each of five dates, in
  the same order as the shapes. The plate answers "which is which, and by how much" without a second
  chart.
- The title states the span, the subtitle states which portion is observation and which is
  projection, and the projected years (`2050 2075 2100`) are the outermost shapes — so *where the
  evidence stops* is legible from the geometry.
- Source, licence and author are on the plate, bottom left and right.

## What is transferable

- **Past two states, drop the bars.** One or two populations can be bars; nine cannot, and the honest
  move is a nested envelope rather than nine overlaid bar sets. This is the family's upper bound,
  measured.
- **Mirroring about a shared zero with both halves named in words** — corroborating
  PopulationPyramid.net and ONS from a third publication.
- **The unit inside the tick** (`70 Million`, `10 years`) rather than in an axis title.
- **Direct labels on the shapes, on both halves, no legend.** Nine series and not one key. The
  labelling cost is paid twice so that neither half has to be read against the other.
- **A ramp used as time**, with the projected years at the light end, so "this part has not happened
  yet" is legible before the caption is read.
- **A margin list that ranks what the shapes only imply** — median age per date, in shape order.

## What was not verified

- **The plate's own type.** Raster `img`, no `graphicFrame`; all type in the record is the article's.
- **Whether the ramp is a documented OWID scale or this plate's own.** Nothing in the record says.
- **Where observation ends and projection begins, on the picture.** The subtitle says 2018; the plate
  does not visibly change treatment at that boundary, and this note does not claim it does.
- **The `#FEFEFE` at 36.04 %.** That is the paper *inside the plate's frame* as photographed; the
  figure is stated as an observation, not used as a direction.
- The page was read **after a consent dialog was dismissed** (`"Accept optional cookies"`).
