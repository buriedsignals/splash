# 100 datavizproject — #1, stacked column

- archive: datavizproject
- type: stacked column
- export: static
- readAs: the encoding as the page draws it, read at rest

## What it is

The first of a hundred encodings of one fixed dataset — World Heritage sites for Norway, Denmark
and Sweden, in 2004 and 2022. Two stacked columns, one per year, each country a segment.

## What it does with information

**The value is printed on the mark.** Every segment carries its own number, in white, inside the
segment. No axis reading is required to know that Sweden went 13 → 15.

**The total is stated separately from the parts.** 22 and 33 sit above their columns in bold. A
stacked column normally makes the reader add its segments; here the sum is given, so the part *and*
the whole are both readable without arithmetic.

**Identity is a direct label in the mark's own colour.** "Sweden", "Denmark", "Norway" sit at the
right of their segments, each set in that segment's own hue, instead of in a detached legend.

## What it does with style

One house style across all hundred: a blue / vermillion / navy triad, a white plot on pale grey
paper, values in white inside marks and in dark grey outside. Two families, no italic, tracking only
in the site's own navigation. The palette is deliberately constant so that the *encoding* is the
only variable across the hundred — which is precisely what makes this archive useful for treatments
and useless for directions.

## What is transferable

- **Print the value on the mark** whenever the series is short enough that every mark can carry one.
- **State the total beside the stack**, so a part-to-whole never demands mental addition.
- **Label identity in the mark's own colour**, at the mark, rather than in a legend.

## What is this piece's own

The triad of hues and the pale grey page: Ferdio's house style, held constant on purpose.

## What was not verified

Interaction, motion, and the narrow-window layout. Read at 1440×900, at rest.
