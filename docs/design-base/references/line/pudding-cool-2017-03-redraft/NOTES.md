## What it is

Every NBA draft pick over twenty years, each drawn as a stem measuring how far its "redrafted" rank
sits from where it was actually taken. The pixel route reads the palette as **diverging**, two poles
at 345° and 40° — pink above, amber below.

## What it does with information

**Each entry is measured against its own expected position, not against the extremes.** The stem
runs from a diagonal reference — actual pick equals redraft pick — so a mid-table pick that moved
twenty places reads as remarkable, which it is, while a first pick that could not move reads as
ordinary, which it also is. Compared to the top of the list, both would look the same.

**The two directions of deviation get the two poles of a diverging palette.** Over-performing and
under-performing are opposite signs of one quantity, and the colour says which sign without a
legend.

**Emphasis is reserved for length relative to neighbours**, not for absolute rank: the longest stems
cluster in the middle of the draft because that is where there is room to move.

## What it does with style

Ground `#FFFFFF` measured on the graphic element, with the two poles of the diverging palette as
the only chromatic ink on it. The per-pick labels are set small and in the quiet register, so a list
of two hundred names reads as texture until one is sought.

*(An earlier draft of this note asserted a near-black ground. It was wrong — a number written before
it was checked, in a corpus whose whole value is that its numbers are measured. Corrected against

## What is transferable

- **Anchor each entry to its own local expectation** when a ranking's interesting cases are not at
  the extremes. This is `reference-set.md`'s own lesson for this piece, and the encoding above is
  how it is drawn.
- **A diverging palette for a signed deviation**, one pole per direction, never a sequential ramp.

The sport, and Pudding's pink-and-amber pair.
