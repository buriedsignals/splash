- readAs: the published poster as the page serves it, read at rest. **Harvested twice:** the first

## What it is

American political worldviews laid out as two mirrored halves of one diagram — Left on the blue
side, Right on the red — with the Capitol dome at the centre and matched rows running outward:
parties, values, the economy, society and culture, family, beliefs.

## What it does with information

**The two poles share one structure, not two charts.** Every row exists on both sides at the same
vertical position, so a reader compares by moving horizontally across a single line — "TRADE: fair
trade" against "TRADE: free trade" — instead of holding one side in memory while reading the other.
The mirror is the mechanism.

**The centre is the shared object, not a gap.** The Capitol sits on the axis of symmetry and both
sides' arrows point at it. What the two disagree about is drawn once, in the middle, rather than
twice.

**The tint carries membership; the outline carries the claim.** Zone fills are pale — the pixel
route measures the tints at 46–49 % chroma against the poles' 83 % — so the coloured wash says
"this region belongs to that side" without competing with the labelled boxes and rules that carry
the actual statements.

## What it does with style

Ground `#FEFEFE`, palette **diverging**, two hue clusters at 18° (warm red) and 207° (blue), each
with its own lightness ramp — pole, mid tint, pale zone fill. Three families; thirteen letter-spaced
runs, eight case-transformed. Labels sit in drawn banners and boxes, not free on the ground.

## What is transferable

- **For a two-pole comparison, mirror one structure rather than juxtaposing two charts.** The
  matched vertical position is what turns a comparison into a single horizontal read.
- **Draw the shared object once, on the axis of symmetry.** A gap between two halves says they have
  nothing in common; a shared centre says what they are arguing about.
- **A pole and its tint ramp, per side.** Membership is a pale fill, the claim is a labelled mark;
  the two never compete because they are an order of magnitude apart in chroma.
- **Set labels in their own drawn container** — a banner, a box — when they sit over tinted zones,
  so their legibility does not depend on the fill beneath them.

## What is this piece's own

The blue/red assignment, which is a US convention and inverts in several other countries; the
Capitol as the centre; the line-art illustration.

## What was not verified

The claims themselves — this note describes the encoding, not the politics. The narrow-window
layout. And whether the newsletter modal that contaminated the first capture appears on a schedule
or at random; it did not reappear on the second visit.
