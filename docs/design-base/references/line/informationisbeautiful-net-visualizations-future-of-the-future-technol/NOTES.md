# Information is Beautiful — "The Future of the Future"

- url: https://informationisbeautiful.net/visualizations/future-of-the-future-technological-predictions-made-about-the-future-in-2009/
- archive: informationisbeautiful
- type: annotated timeline, icon marks
- export: static
- readAs: the published poster as the page serves it, read at rest

## What it is

Predictions made in 2009 about the future, plotted against a horizontal axis of years from 2010 to
2100. Each prediction is a small icon with a caption set beside it. A second register runs along the
baseline: things predicted to be **extinct** by each date — rental stores, DVDs, post offices,
computer mice, the Aral Sea, glaciers, physical newspapers, passports.

## What it does with information

**Two classes of claim, two registers, one axis.** Arrivals are icons in the field; disappearances
are a text band at the baseline. The reader can follow either without the other interfering, and
both are read against the same dates. A single mark type would have forced the two to compete.

**The mark depicts its own subject.** Each prediction's icon is drawn from what it predicts — a
rocket, a brain, a coin, a camera — so a reader scanning the field gets the domain before reading
the caption.

**The axis is dated and irregular in density.** Predictions crowd the near years and thin toward
2100, and the layout lets them: nothing is spaced evenly to look tidy. The crowding *is* a finding —
2009 believed far more about the next fifteen years than about the next eighty.

## What it does with style

Ground `#EBE6E5`; the pixel route reads **diverging**, two poles at 32° and 348°. The field is a
quarter-ellipse gradient running cyan → magenta → yellow, bounded by an arc rather than a
rectangular frame.

**And that gradient is worth naming honestly**, because it is exactly what this repository's own
`doctrine/references/visual-system.md` forbids: *"no texture, no vignette, no gradient wash behind
the marks … any gradient or texture on the ground competes with the marks for the reader's contrast
budget."* The wash here is doing something — distance from now — but it is doing it weakly, and the
icon captions sitting on the magenta band are measurably the hardest text on the poster to read.
This is a reference where **the doctrine is right and the reference is wrong**, and it is filed that
way rather than laundered.

## What is transferable

- **Two classes of claim on one axis, in two registers** — a field and a baseline band — instead of
  one mark type carrying both.
- **Let density on the axis carry a finding.** Irregular crowding is information; evening it out for
  tidiness destroys it.
- **An icon mark that depicts its own subject**, where the entities are concrete enough to draw.

## What is this piece's own — and what should not be lifted

The gradient wash. It is the piece's signature and it costs legibility; see above.

## What was not verified

Whether any prediction is marked as having come true — some icons carry a starburst whose meaning is
not stated in the part of the poster that was read. Nothing above depends on it.
