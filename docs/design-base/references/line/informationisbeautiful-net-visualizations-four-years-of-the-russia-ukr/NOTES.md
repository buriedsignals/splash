# Information is Beautiful — "Four Years of the Russia-Ukraine War"

- url: https://informationisbeautiful.net/visualizations/four-years-of-the-russia-ukraine-war/
- archive: informationisbeautiful
- type: unit chart (isotype), with a comparison panel
- export: static
- readAs: the published poster as the page serves it, read at rest, together with its own printed
  captions and its unit key

## What it is

Casualties in the Russia-Ukraine war drawn as human figures, one figure per thousand people, on
black. The left panel is the subject — Ukrainian killed ~90,000 in yellow, Ukrainian wounded
~420,000 in gold, Russian losses in cyan below. The right panel, **at the identical unit scale**, is
a set of past conflicts: Chechnya 20,000, Afghanistan 15,000, the Korean War 35,000, Vietnam 60,000,
US losses in the Second World War 405,000.

## What it does with information

**The comparison set shares the subject's unit and is drawn in neutral grey.** This is the single
strongest thing in the piece. The historical conflicts are not a separate chart with its own scale —
they are the same figures, the same size, one thousand people each, laid out beside the subject and
desaturated. The reader compares by counting the same object, and the colour says which one the
piece is about.

**The qualification sits with the mark it qualifies.** "(approx. 50% of wounded return to
battlefield)" is printed directly beneath the wounded block, not in a footnote. The number is
never read without its caveat because the caveat is inside the graphic.

**The magnitude is stated twice, in two registers**: as a headline number in the display register
("Over **1.6 Million** Casualties") and as a countable field of marks. Neither alone would do the
work — the number is unreadable as a quantity, the field is unreadable as a figure.

## What it does with style

Ground `#000000`; the pixel route reads the palette as **categorical, three poles** (187°, 50°,
342°). A black ground with saturated marks and a full grey ramp for context. Two families, one
italic run, fifteen letter-spaced runs, eight case-transformed.

## What is transferable

- **Draw the comparison set at the subject's own unit and desaturate it.** A shared unit makes a
  historical comparison honest in a way two charts on two scales never can, and the neutral/accent
  split says which is the subject without a word.
- **Print the caveat inside the graphic, adjacent to the mark it qualifies**, so the number cannot
  be read without it.
- **State a large magnitude both as a figure and as a countable field.** They answer different
  questions and neither substitutes for the other.

## What is this piece's own

The human pictogram, the specific hues, the black ground.
