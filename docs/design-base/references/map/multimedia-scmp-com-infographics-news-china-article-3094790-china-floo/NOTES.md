## What it is

A map of China locating the Yangtze basin, set inside a longform explainer on the 2020 floods.

## What it does with information

**Three classes of geography, three typographic treatments, on one plate.**

- the country as `CHINA` — **tracked capitals, grey**, the quietest thing on the map;
- the feature under discussion as `Yangtze River basin` — **mixed case, in the piece's orange
  accent**, with a hairline leader to the outlined basin;
- the water itself as `Yangtze River` — **italic, in the river's own blue**.

That is the same three-treatment convention ProPublica's *louisiana-toxic-air* uses on an entirely
different continent, at an entirely independent desk. **Two publications, and it is the pair that
finally evidences the `place` register.**

**The accent names what the story is about, and the basemap names the rest.** The basin outline and
its label are orange; the river is blue because it is water; the country is grey because it is
context. Colour is doing the same job the typography is.

**No borders, no cities, no graticule.** The plate carries the coastline, the basin and the river,
and nothing else. A locator map that named provinces would have made the reader look for one.

## What it does with style

A soft blue glow behind the landmass in place of a sea fill; the country in white with a hairline
outline. Ground reads near-white. The piece above it sets a serif headline against a sans
standfirst.

## What is transferable

- **Country in tracked capitals, feature in mixed case and in the accent, water in italic.** A
  reader separates the three without a key, and the convention is old enough that they already
  carry it.
- **Strip a locator map to the features the sentence names.** Anything else invites a search.
- **Let the water keep its own colour and the subject take the accent** — the two never compete
  because they are answering different questions.

## What is this piece's own

The soft glow behind the landmass, which is decoration rather than encoding.
