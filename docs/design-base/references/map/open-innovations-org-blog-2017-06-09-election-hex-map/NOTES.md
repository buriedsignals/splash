# Open Innovations — 2017 General Election hex map

- url: https://open-innovations.org/blog/2017-06-09-election-hex-map
- archive: search
- type: hex grid, filled live as results arrive, with the undeclared cells left empty
- export: static
- readAs: the blog post's own embedded graphic, a 720 x 450 `<img>`, photographed whole. The type
  figures in `measured.json` are the SITE's — the graphic is a raster.

## What it is

The same 650-hex layout as the sibling record, filled by winning party as the 2017 results came in,
photographed at **643 of 650 results returned**. A categorical key of eleven parties sits above the
map in two rows; a timestamp sits bottom right.

## What it does with information

**The seven seats not yet declared are drawn as EMPTY hexes, not omitted.** They keep their place in
the grid, in outline only, on the page's own grey. The grid stays complete, so a reader can see how
much of the picture is still missing — and where. That is
`a-missing-cell-is-drawn-as-missing` in a hex geometry, and it is the strongest thing this record
carries.

**The plate prints how much of itself is filled**: *"643 of 650 results returned"*, directly under
the key, above the map. The count is not a caption about the data, it is a caption about **the
drawing's own completeness**.

**And it is stamped with the hour** — `As of 06:30:56` — because a plate that will be different in
ten minutes has to say when it was true.

**The key is categorical and sits above the map**, wrapped to two rows, one swatch and one name per
party, in the parties' own colours. Eleven categories is more than any ramp could carry; a hex map of
winners is one of the few forms where that many named hues is the right answer.

## What it does with style

Ground `#EEEEEE` at 74 %, the pixel route reading the fills as **diverging** — an artefact of two
large opposed blocks of blue `#849CEB` and red `#DB7A7C`, not a scale. The empty hexes are the same
grey as the ground with a hairline edge.

## What is transferable

- **An undecided or missing unit keeps its cell and loses its fill.** The grid is the promise; a
  hole in it is information.
- **Print how much of the plate is filled**, as a count of units, next to the key.
- **Stamp a live plate with the moment it was true.**

## What is this piece's own

The party hues; the 2017 result.

## What was not verified

- Whether the empty hexes were visually distinguishable from the seven at the map's edges in the
  live version at other zooms.
