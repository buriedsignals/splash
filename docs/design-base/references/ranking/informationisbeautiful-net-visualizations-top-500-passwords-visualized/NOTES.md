## What it is

The five hundred most common passwords, from breach data, laid out as a cloud in which each
password is set in a type size proportional to how often it appears, and coloured by a category —
alphanumeric, animal, fluffy, food, macho, names, nerdy, rebellious, security, sports.

## What it does with information

**The label is the mark.** There is no dot, bar or cell that a password is attached to: the word
*is* the datum, drawn at the size its rank earns. For a ranking whose entries are short strings this
removes the lookup entirely — a reader scanning for their own password reads the plate rather than
matching a label to a mark.

**The ranking is addressed to the reader in the second person, and that is the way in.** The
subtitle is three words: "Is yours here?" The piece does not ask the reader to admire an order, it
gives them a subject to find — themselves. For a five-hundred-entry list with no numbers, that is
the whole navigation strategy above the fold.

**The category legend is the filter, with its instruction beneath the subtitle** — "select a
category below to filter", set small and italic, then ten labelled swatches in a single row.

**And the price of the form is stated by the form.** No rank number appears anywhere, and area is a
poor scale for comparison across a cloud: `password`, `qwerty`, `letmein` and `123456` are visibly
the largest and nothing tells you which is first. This is a ranking that has traded its ordinal for
its texture, deliberately, and it is worth recording as the trade it is.

## What is transferable

- **Let the label be the mark** when the entries are short strings; the reader finds their subject
  by reading rather than by matching.
- **Address the reader in the second person in the subtitle** when the list is one they might be in.
- **A category legend used as a filter, with its instruction directly under the subtitle.**

## What is this piece's own

The cloud. It is the right form here only because the entries are words, the categories are the
argument and the exact ordinal genuinely does not matter — three conditions a newsroom ranking will
rarely all meet.

## What was not verified

The graphic's own typography, at any size. Whether the size scale is linear, log or ranked. Every
category hue beyond `#CD3029`. The filter's behaviour — nothing was clicked. Whether any rank number
is available on hover.
