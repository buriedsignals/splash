## What it is

Every recognised dog breed placed on two axes: "our data score" horizontally, popularity
vertically. Each breed is drawn as its own silhouette, in the colour of its breed group, labelled
with its name.

## What it does with information

**The composite score shows its ingredients, as a legend, inside the plot.** At the lower left,
under the words "our data score", six icons with labels: intelligence, costs, longevity, grooming,
ailments, appetite. The axis is not asked to be taken on trust — the six things it is made of are
drawn beside it, at the origin, where the axis starts.

**The two-by-two is named, in the display register, at the corners.** "Inexplicably Overrated" top
left; "Hot Dogs!" top right. A reader gets the verdict for a region of the plot before reading a
single breed, and the ranking's argument — that popularity and merit come apart — is carried by two
phrases rather than by a paragraph.

**The mark depicts its subject and the mark also carries two more variables.** The silhouette is the
breed; its *drawn size* is the breed's size and its *rendering* is the intelligence band, both
declared in a glyph legend at the top right that shows the same silhouette at `dumb` / `clever` and
`sml` / `med` / `lrge`. A legend that teaches a glyph by drawing the glyph, not by describing it.

**Colour is the breed group and nothing else**, with a seven-entry legend across the top right —
Herding, Hound, Non-sporting, Sporting, Terrier, Toy, Working — each set in its own hue rather than
paired with a swatch.

## What it does with style

Ground `#F7F1E1` at **69.24 %** coverage (pixel route) — a warm paper cream, the piece's most
consequential decision, since the silhouettes are muted earth hues that would go flat on white. The
style route reports the page body as `rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)`, so the ground is the pixel route's alone.

The pixel palette's **shared** entries — `#D4537A` 0.696 %, `#EAAB4A` 0.683 %, `#ECB445` 0.651 %,
`#E69B53` 0.635 %, `#DE7B64` 0.630 %, `#E28B5B` 0.627 %, `#D65C76` 0.612 % — are bit-identical
across all four informationisbeautiful.net records in this wave and belong to the site's
promotional banner, not the graphic. This record's own three unique entries are plausibly the
plot's: `#A13330` at 0.79 % (a brick red), `#D7856A` at 0.71 % (a clay), `#50447A` at 0.70 % (a
muted violet). Palette shape read as `diverging`, which is an artefact of the banner's two poles
rather than a reading of the plot.

The axes are drawn as two thin black rules with arrowheads, crossing at the origin, with no ticks
and no gridlines — the plot is a field of silhouettes and two lines.

## What is transferable

- **Draw the composite's ingredients as a legend at the origin of the axis they make.**
- **Name the quadrants in the display register** when the argument is that two dimensions disagree.
- **Teach a glyph by drawing the glyph** at both ends of its range, rather than describing it.
- **Set legend entries in their own colours** instead of pairing a swatch with neutral text.

## What is this piece's own

The subject, the hand-drawn silhouettes, and the cream. The named quadrants are also editorially
loaded in a way a newsroom would need to own: "Inexplicably Overrated" is a judgement, printed at
display scale, over named breeds.

## What was not verified

The graphic's own typography. The plot's own hues, beyond the three chromatic entries not shared
with the other three records. Whether the piece is interactive. Whether "our data score" is a
weighted or unweighted combination of the six components — the legend names them and does not weight
them.
