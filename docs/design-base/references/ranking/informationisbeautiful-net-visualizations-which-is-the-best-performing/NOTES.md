# Information is Beautiful — "Which is The Best Performing Marvel Movie?"

- url: https://informationisbeautiful.net/visualizations/which-is-the-best-performing-marvel-movie/
- archive: informationisbeautiful
- type: ranking whose criterion is a reader control
- export: web
- readAs: the published graphic as the page serves it, read at rest in the 1440×900 screenshot. The
  graphic is embedded; the **style route measured the page wrapper only**. The pixel route's
  chromatic list is the site's promo banner — see below.

## What it is

Every Marvel film plotted by release year against percentage of budget recovered, each point
directly labelled, with the franchise names across the top acting as a filter and three dropdown
pills along the bottom edge.

## What it does with information

**The ranking's criterion is a control, and the control states the criterion in force.** Three
pills sit on the graphic's bottom edge reading `size: no metric`, `y-axis: % budget recovered` and
`x-axis: year`. Each names its own current value in its own label. A reader who arrives mid-page,
or who returns after changing something, can read what the order means off the control itself — no
memory, no separate caption. A pill labelled only "sort" or "options" would not do this.

That is the whole answer to *what is a ranking of*: the piece declines to pick one, offers the
choice, and keeps the answer visible.

**The legend is the filter and the piece says so** — `USE THE LEGEND TO FILTER`, set immediately
under the title, above a row of nine franchise names each in its own hue: ANT-MAN, AVENGERS, BLACK
PANTHER, CAPTAIN AMERICA, DR STRANGE, GUARDIANS, IRON MAN, SPIDER-MAN, THOR, UNIQUE. The legend
entries are set in the franchise colour rather than paired with a swatch, so the row is both key and
control and spends no extra ink on either.

**A named threshold, drawn.** A dashed horizontal rule labelled `BREAKEVEN` at the left margin. On a
"% budget recovered" scale the meaningful line is not zero and not the median: it is 100 %, and the
piece draws it and names it in words rather than leaving the reader to find `100%` on the axis.

**Every point is labelled directly**, in its franchise's colour. Identity is never a hover.

## What it does with style

Ground `#26232C` at **78.31 %** coverage (pixel route) — a desaturated near-black with a violet
cast. The style route reports the page body as `rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)`, so the ground is the pixel
route's alone.

**None of this record's measured chromatic palette is the graphic.** Its top ten `(hex, share)`
pairs are identical, to five decimal places, to those of the LLM record harvested in the same wave —
`#D4537A` 0.696 %, `#EAAB4A` 0.683 %, `#ECB445` 0.651 %, and so on — because they are the site's
own pink-to-amber promotional banner above every piece, not the plot. Two different graphics cannot
have the same palette to five decimals; the arithmetic is what caught it. No hue from the plot is
quoted here.

## What is transferable

- **A re-rank control that names the criterion currently in force**, sitting on the graphic's own
  edge.
- **A named threshold rule** where the scale has a meaningful level that is not its zero.
- **A legend set in its own colours, used as a filter, with the instruction beside it.**

## What is this piece's own

The dark violet ground and IIB's franchise palette. The scatter form — this is a ranking that has
chosen to keep a time axis, which most rankings have no reason to.

## What was not verified

The graphic's own typography. Every hue in the plot. The three pills' behaviour and what other
criteria they offer — nothing was clicked, so it is not known whether changing the y-axis re-labels
the `BREAKEVEN` rule or removes it. Whether the ranking is ever presented as an ordered list rather
than a scatter.
