# The world's largest economies by nominal GDP

The global economy is dominated by a handful of national heavyweights. According to
International Monetary Fund estimates, the world's total nominal output stands at roughly
**$126.3 trillion**.

The **United States** remains the single largest economy at about **$32.4 trillion**,
followed by **China** at **$20.9 trillion**. Together these two economies account for
roughly a quarter of all global output — more than the next eight combined.

A steep drop separates the top two from the rest of the pack. **Germany** leads the
chasing group at **$5.5 trillion**, ahead of the **United Kingdom** ($4.3tn), **Japan**
($4.4tn) and **India** ($4.2tn). **France** ($3.6tn), **Italy** ($2.7tn), **Brazil**
($2.6tn) and **Canada** ($2.5tn) round out the ten largest.

## Provenance note (workflow test case 28)

This case was originally briefed against the Visual Capitalist article
*"The $117 Trillion World Economy in 2025"*
(`https://www.visualcapitalist.com/117-trillion-world-economy-in-2025/`). That URL
returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden**, and every archive/cache/proxy fallback
(Google cache, web.archive.org, archive.ph, 12ft.io) was unreachable from this
environment. **No figures were extracted from it, and none were fabricated.**

The figures above are transcribed verbatim from the fetchable substitute source:
Wikipedia, **"List of countries by GDP (nominal)"**
(`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal)`), which reports
**IMF estimates labelled (2026)** — *not* the article's 2025 numbers, and the world
total on this source is **$126.3 trillion**, not the $117tn of the original headline.
