# The world's container trade still runs through a handful of Asian mega-ports

Global container shipping is strikingly concentrated. In 2024, seven of the ten
busiest container ports on Earth were in China, led by Shanghai, which alone handled
more than 49 million twenty-foot-equivalent units (TEU) — more than the next two ports
combined. Singapore, the great trans-shipment hub of Southeast Asia, ranked second
with about 39 million.

Europe's largest gateway, Rotterdam, moved roughly 14 million TEU — a fraction of
Shanghai's throughput. The United States' busiest complex, Los Angeles/Long Beach,
handled around 19 million between its two adjacent ports.

The story is one of magnitude at specific places: where the boxes actually move,
and how lopsidedly that volume clusters on the western Pacific rim.

Source: Lloyd's List / port authority throughput figures, 2024.
