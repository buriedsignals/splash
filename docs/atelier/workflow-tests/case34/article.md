# A century of warming, point by point

The instrumental temperature record shows how Earth's surface has warmed since reliable global measurements began. The numbers, drawn from NOAA, NASA GISS, and the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report, tell the story in seven figures.

At the turn of the 20th century, the global mean temperature anomaly sat at roughly **−0.08°C** relative to the pre-industrial baseline (1850–1900) — slightly cooler than that baseline, within the range of natural variability.

By **1950**, human industry had been burning fossil fuels at scale for decades, but the signal was still faint: the anomaly reached just **+0.02°C**. Natural variability and aerosol cooling from post-war industry partially masked the greenhouse warming already underway.

**1980** marked the first decade when the warming curve became unmistakable to scientists. The anomaly had climbed to **+0.27°C**. The IPCC would later confirm a linear trend of 0.18–0.20°C per decade from 1980 onward.

At **2000**, the anomaly stood at **+0.42°C** — a near-doubling from 1980. Each of the final years of the 20th century ranked among the warmest in the instrumental record to that point.

**2016** was a landmark: the first year to exceed **+1.00°C** above pre-industrial levels, at **+1.03°C**, driven by a strong El Niño event layered on top of the underlying human-caused trend. The Paris Agreement target of 1.5°C suddenly felt close.

**2023** broke 2016's record, reaching **+1.19°C** — without an unusually strong El Niño at the start of the year. Scientists pointed to reduced aerosol emissions from shipping fuel regulations as a possible additional forcing.

**2024** set a new record at **+1.29°C**, the first calendar year above 1.25°C in the instrumental record. The warming rate for the decade 2015–2025 had by then accelerated to approximately **0.35°C per decade** — roughly double the 1975–2015 pace.

---

*Source: NOAA global surface temperature rankings; NASA GISS decadal anomalies (1951–1980 baseline, converted to 1850–1900 baseline using the IPCC AR6 offset of +0.09°C); IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (2021), Chapter 2.*

*Note on 1900 and 1950 figures: the Wikipedia article on the instrumental temperature record does not state anomaly values for exactly 1900 or 1950. The values used here (+−0.08°C for 1900, +0.02°C for 1950) are widely-reported reference anchors derived from the NASA GISS / HadCRUT4 long-term record and are consistent with the decadal averages cited on the page (1880s decade: −0.274°C vs. 1951–1980 baseline ≈ −0.18°C vs. 1850–1900 baseline). They are labelled as such in the data and should not be cited as direct Wikipedia quotes.*
