# Marriage equality went global one country at a time — watch it spread across the map

When the Netherlands opened marriage to same-sex couples on 1 April 2001, it stood alone. No
other country on Earth granted the right. A quarter of a century later, 36 countries — from
Argentina to Taiwan, from Iceland to South Africa — have followed. This is not a story about a
single number. It is a story about a **wave**: where the change started, how it moved, and the
order in which the map filled in.

The point of this piece is the **sequence and the spread**, not any one country's date. Read the
map the way the last 25 years actually unfolded: watch the first pin drop in Western Europe, watch
it jump to a different continent, and watch the pace change as the idea diffused.

## A slow start in Western Europe

For the first years, the change was almost entirely a Low Countries and Iberian affair. Belgium
followed the Netherlands in 2003. Spain and Canada — the first outside Europe — arrived together in
2005. Then, in 2006, the wave made its most surprising early jump: **South Africa** became the first
and, for years, the only African country to recognise same-sex marriage. Geography, not chronology
alone, is the story here: the idea was not spreading outward from one epicentre in neat rings. It was
leaping between continents.

## The Nordic and Southern-Cone clusters

Between 2009 and 2013 two distinct geographic clusters lit up almost in parallel. In the north,
Norway and Sweden (2009), then Iceland and Denmark, turned Scandinavia into a solid bloc. In the
south, **Argentina** (2010) and then Uruguay and Brazil (2013) did the same for the Southern Cone of
South America — the first region outside Europe to fill in as a cluster. Watching the two clusters
appear on opposite hemispheres in the same handful of years is the payoff: it is a pattern you feel
as motion, not as a table of dates.

## 2015: the tipping point

2015 was the inflection year. Luxembourg, Ireland — the first country to legalise by popular vote —
and the **United States**, via a Supreme Court ruling that flipped the entire country at once, all
arrived within months. After 2015 the pace accelerated: Colombia (2016); Finland, Malta, Germany and
Australia (2017). The map was no longer filling one pin at a time; whole years now brought several
countries at once.

## The wave reaches Asia — and keeps moving

In 2019 **Taiwan** became the first place in Asia to recognise same-sex marriage — another
cross-continental leap, not an outward ripple. The 2020s brought Ecuador and Costa Rica (2020);
Switzerland, Chile, Slovenia and Cuba (2022); Andorra (2023); Estonia and Greece (2024); and, in
2025, Liechtenstein and — the first in Southeast Asia — **Thailand**. Twenty-five years after the
Netherlands stood alone, the map tells a story a single chart cannot: a right that began at one point
and diffused, unevenly and by continental leaps, across the whole world.

The order in which the map fills in — start, jumps, clusters, tipping point, and the leap into Asia
— is the article. This is a guided walk through 36 states of one evolving map.
