# The slow, partial return to the office

No dataset accompanies this piece — the numbers are woven through the prose, the way a
reporter files copy. The workflow's job is to pull the quantified claims out of the text.

When offices emptied in 2020, few predicted how sticky remote work would prove. Badge-swipe
data from large US employers tells a story of a slow, incomplete return. Average office
occupancy sat at just 30% of pre-pandemic levels at the start of 2021. It crept to 43% by
2022, reached 50% in 2023, and 54% in 2024 — before settling at 58% in 2025. Five years
on, offices remain barely more than half full.

The hybrid compromise has hardened into the norm: most large employers now mandate roughly
three days a week on site, and the Tuesday-to-Thursday "peak" is visibly busier than the
bookended Mondays and Fridays.

Source: Kastle Systems badge-swipe occupancy barometer (illustrative), 2021–2025.
