# Sawe shatters the two-hour wall as the marathon record keeps falling

For decades, running a marathon in under two hours was treated as a physiological impossibility. On 26 April 2026, in London, Kenya's Sabastian Sawe made it official: 1:59:30, the first sub-two-hour finish ever recorded in a record-eligible race.

Sawe's mark shaved 65 seconds off the previous world record of 2:00:35, set by his compatriot Kelvin Kiptum in Chicago in 2023. That drop is one of the largest single improvements the event has seen in the modern era, and it caps a startling run of progress. The record has now fallen three times in four years.

The story of that acceleration is written in the numbers. When Paul Tergat clocked 2:04:55 in Berlin in 2003, it was the first officially ratified time under 2:05. It took Haile Gebrselassie two more Berlin runs — 2:04:26 in 2007 and 2:03:59 in 2008 — to push through 2:04. Patrick Makau (2:03:38 in 2011), Wilson Kipsang (2:03:23 in 2013) and Dennis Kimetto (2:02:57 in 2014) chipped away in turn, each shaving seconds rather than minutes.

Then came Eliud Kipchoge. His 2:01:39 in Berlin in 2018 was the biggest leap in a generation, and he lowered his own record to 2:01:09 in 2022. Kiptum's 2:00:35 in 2023 brought the barrier within touching distance before his death in early 2024. Sawe finally crossed it.

The geography of the record is as striking as its pace. Of the last ten marks, Kenya has held seven, with Ethiopia's Gebrselassie accounting for two and the rest also Kenyan. Berlin's flat, fast course produced eight of those records; only Kiptum's Chicago run and Sawe's London breakthrough came elsewhere.

From Tergat's 2:04:55 to Sawe's 1:59:30, the world record has come down by five minutes and 25 seconds in a little over two decades — proof that a wall long thought immovable was only waiting for the right runner on the right day.
