# More health spending buys longer lives — until the US

Across rich countries, higher health spending broadly tracks longer lives. But the
United States is the glaring exception: it spends far more per person than anyone else —
about $12,500 a year — yet its life expectancy of 76 is among the lowest in the group,
below countries spending a third as much. Japan, Spain and South Korea reach 84 on a
fraction of the American outlay.

Source: OECD Health Statistics, 2023.
