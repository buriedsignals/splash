# The China–Europe rail corridor: 11,000 km without a ship

No dataset accompanies this piece — it is a geographic explainer of a route.

Every week, hundreds of block trains leave the inland Chinese city of Chongqing and
head not east to the coast, but west — overland, across the whole of Eurasia. The
"New Silk Road" rail corridor threads out of western China at the Alashankou border,
runs the length of Kazakhstan, crosses Russia and Belarus, changes gauge at the
Polish frontier of Brest, and finally reaches the Rhine at Duisburg, Germany — the
inland port that has become the corridor's western anchor.

The journey covers roughly 11,000 kilometres in 12 to 16 days: slower than a plane,
far faster than the five to six weeks a container spends at sea via the Suez Canal, and
independent of the maritime chokepoints. It passes through six countries and two
gauge changes, but never touches an ocean.

Source: geographic references from Natural Earth; corridor described from UTLC / DB Cargo route maps.
