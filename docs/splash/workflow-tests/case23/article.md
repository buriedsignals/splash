Spending more on health does not buy longer lives. The United States spends about $12,500 per person each year, more than anyone, yet Americans live to just 76. Japan spends a little over $4,700 and reaches 84. Germany, at $7,400, gets to 81, much like the United Kingdom at $5,400. Even Korea, spending only $3,600, reaches 83 — while Mexico, at $1,200, sits near the bottom at 75. The pattern is a scatter with no upward slope: past a modest level, more money adds no years.
