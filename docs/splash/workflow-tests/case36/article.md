# Enlargement of the European Union

The European Union traces its origin to the Treaty of Rome (1957), which came into force on 1 January 1958 with six founding members: Belgium, France, West Germany, Italy, Luxembourg, and the Netherlands.

Since then the Union has grown through seven successive enlargements, each adding a new wave of member states.

**1973 — Northern enlargement:** Denmark, Ireland, and the United Kingdom joined, bringing total membership to nine. The UK later left (Brexit, 2020) but its 1973 accession year remains the relevant data point for this diffusion story.

**1981 — Southern enlargement:** Greece acceded, becoming the tenth member state.

**1986 — Iberian enlargement:** Portugal and Spain joined simultaneously.

**1995 — EFTA enlargement:** Austria, Finland, and Sweden — former EFTA neutrals — entered after the Cold War ended.

**2004 — Eastern enlargement (largest single wave):** Ten states joined at once — Cyprus, the Czech Republic, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, Poland, Slovakia, and Slovenia — the biggest single expansion in the Union's history.

**2007 — Balkan/Black Sea enlargement:** Bulgaria and Romania acceded.

**2013 — Most recent enlargement:** Croatia became the 28th member state on 1 July 2013. The UK's subsequent withdrawal (2020) reduced current membership to 27.
