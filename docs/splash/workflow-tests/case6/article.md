# Southern Europe now swelters through dozens of tropical nights a year

A "tropical night" — when the temperature never drops below 20°C — used to be rare
north of the Mediterranean. No longer. In 2024, parts of southern Spain, Italy and
Greece recorded more than 60 tropical nights, while much of Scandinavia and the
British Isles still saw almost none.

The pattern is sharply geographic: a hot Mediterranean rim shading to a cool north.
Malta led the continent with 82 nights above 20°C; Greece and Cyprus followed in the
70s. France, straddling the divide, sat near 24. Norway, Sweden and Ireland barely
registered, with fewer than three.

Tropical nights matter for health: without the relief of nighttime cooling, heat
stress accumulates, and mortality climbs among the elderly and the ill.

Source: Copernicus / E-OBS climate reanalysis, national meteorological services, 2024.
