# Mobile-first portrait — the verdict, after opening them

`MOBILE-FIRST-MEASUREMENTS.md` is generated by `mobile-first-probe.mjs`. This file is not: it is what
a person saw when the PNGs were opened, which is the half of the probe a counter cannot do — and on
this round the counters were blind to four separate defects, every one of which was found by looking.

The design under test is `MOBILE-FIRST-WIREFRAME.md`, written before any of this was code.

Open, side by side, in this order:

- `m-h-mobile-first.png` beside `h-b2-story-type.png` and `h-v3-safe-band-centred.png`
- `m-l-mobile-first.png` beside `m-l-b2-story-type.png` and `l-v3-safe-band-centred.png`

`m-l-b2-story-type.png` is new and exists so the line's comparison is fair: the earlier probe never
typeset the line for a phone, so without it the mobile-first line would be compared against an
article-column layout and would win for the wrong reason.

---

## The short answer

**The owner is right, and the earlier probe's own numbers say so once they are divided by three.**

| | verdict |
|---|---|
| histogram | **mobile-first beats both.** Every size on the phone is legible; the prior best has 22 runs at 8–9 CSS px |
| line | **mobile-first beats both**, and the win is larger, because the line's furniture is what was illegible |

And the finding that decides it, which is one division:

> A 1080×1920 story image is shown **full-bleed** on a phone, so **1 frame px = 1/3 CSS px** at a
> 360 dp width. `h-b2-story-type` — the arm `PORTRAIT-VERDICT.md` called "the only one of the four I
> would publish" — draws its axis labels at 25 px and its source at 27 px. **That is 8.3 and 9 CSS
> px on the phone**, below every floor any of the three cited sources will defend. It was praised
> because it was read at desktop size. Its multipliers grew the *header*; the parts a reader has to
> squint at were never touched.

And the finding I did not expect, which is the one that should change the spec:

> **At phone type sizes the 979 px stage holds a headline, a chart and a credit. That is all.** The
> ladder removed the axis title, then the standfirst's second sentence, then all three annotations,
> then the standfirst entirely — on **both** types — and the block only then fitted. The earlier
> probe's central conclusion, that the portrait frame's leftover height should be spent on editorial
> furniture, **inverts** once the words are set at a size a phone can read. There is no leftover.
> The words were the leftover.

## What I saw, arm by arm

### The histogram

**`m-h-mobile-first` (1.86:1 plot, 24% fill, 51 editorial words, nothing under the floor).** A
three-line headline at 72 px, "Number of countries" as a 39 px caption, ten bins, seven gridlines, a
median rule, an x axis and a credit. The decay reads as a decay — 127, 52, 15, 9, and a tail that is
visible rather than implied — and every label is comfortable at arm's length. The bottom 35% of the
frame is empty, and that is not a hole: it is where Instagram puts the caption, the buttons and the
progress bar. On a blank page it looks like a poster with a large margin; on a phone it looks like a
post.

Two residual blemishes, both small and both real: **"Median 3.1 t" sits level with the "120" tick
label and crowds the top gridline** — legible, but it is the same crowding the earlier probe noticed
and the ladder cannot fix it, because moving that label recovers no budget (see below); and the
final x tick reads "40 t" where the last bin is "36 and above", which is inherited from the beat and
not introduced here.

**`h-b2-story-type` beside it.** Handsome at desktop size, and that is the trap. Its title top edge
is at 93 px — inside Meta's 269 px profile-row reserve — and its ink runs to 1852 px, so **780 px of
it is outside the safe band**: the annotations that make it good are exactly the part the interface
sits on. Twenty of its runs are at 25 px. Held next to the mobile-first arm at phone size it is not
a close call.

**`h-v3-safe-band-centred` beside it.** The centring verdict's recommendation, and it is composed
correctly — 11 px of overflow, everything else inside the band. Its smallest type is **16 px = 5.3
CSS px**. It is a well-placed graphic that a phone cannot read.

### The line — where the win is larger and the loss is sharper

**`m-l-mobile-first` (1.63:1 plot, 27% fill, 35 editorial words, nothing under the floor).** The
descent reads as a descent. The 2017 dip and the 2021 and 2023 bumps are proportionate to it, the
break at 2019 is visibly a break with "no data 2019" beside it at 13 CSS px, and "604 mm" sits clear
of the path at the plot's right edge. This is the one I would publish.

**But its end-to-end slope is 28.4°**, against Cleveland's banking-to-45° target and against the
47.7° that `l-v3-safe-band-centred` achieves. The shipped portrait render draws this series at 65.2°
— a drift as a cliff. **The mobile-first arm now draws it at 28.4° — a drift as almost nothing.**
The budget over-corrected. It did so legally: the plot is at 1.63:1, inside the type's declared
0.8–1.8 range, so nothing here is out of bounds; but "inside the range" and "banked correctly" are
not the same constraint, and for a line only the second one is about the reader's understanding.
**This is the sharpest argument in the probe for `PORTRAIT-VERDICT.md`'s own suggestion that a line's
constraint should be stated as a slope target rather than an aspect range.**

**`m-l-b2-story-type` beside it.** This is the fairest test the earlier approach gets, and as a
*page* it is the best-looking artefact in the folder: 117 editorial words, three annotations that
actually say the thing ("It is a drift, not a collapse: the largest year-on-year change is 89 mm"),
a slope banked at 45.3°. And on a phone: axis labels at 25 px, source at 23 px, and **776 px of ink
outside the safe band** — the whole annotation block and the credit sit under the caption and the
buttons, and the headline sits under the profile row. Everything that makes it good is either
unreadable or covered. That is the entire case for mobile-first in one image pair.

## The four defects the instruments could not see

Every arm in the table scores **0 clipped, 0 collisions**. All four of these were in arms that scored
zero, and all four were found by opening the file.

1. **The standfirst was truncated mid-sentence** — "Per-country distribution for 2023 — each of" —
   because the first draft capped it at N wrapped *lines*. That is the same class of defect as a
   chart that truncates its own data: the reader gets a fragment with no sign that it is one. Fixed
   by dropping whole sentences. **A ladder may remove a sentence; it may never cut one in half.**
2. **The line's end label was laid across the line.** The label is right-aligned at the plot's edge
   and was lifted a fixed amount above the *final point*; the segments it spans are higher than that
   point, so it sat on them. The collision counter compares text with **text**, so ink over a path
   scores zero. Fixed by taking the lift from the highest thing the label actually spans — and then
   fixed properly by making the short form the default, since "the sample town 604 mm" repeats the
   headline's own subject and costs 500 px of plot width to do it.
3. **The unit caption overlapped the top tick label** on the line, by 11 px, because `.nice()` puts
   that series' 950 exactly on the plot's top edge. This one *is* text-on-text and would have been
   caught — by a run made after the caption existed. It is in the list because it shows how narrow
   the window is between "the counter cannot see this" and "the counter was not run yet".
4. **Reclassifying the histogram destroyed its claim.** Ten bins into six merged 0–4 and 4–8 into a
   single 179-country column and the right-skew vanished. The counters were perfectly happy: nothing
   clipped, nothing collided, plot inside range. **Only opening it showed that the chart had stopped
   saying anything.**

## Where I disagree with the brief, and with my own wireframe

**1 — "Balance the space" and "use the platform's safe band" are in tension, and the band wins only
if the post really is a story post.** 35% of this frame is deliberately empty. On Instagram that is
correct: the space is occupied by the interface. As a bare PNG — which is what a journalist opens,
and what any other portrait surface receives — **it looks like a graphic that stopped early**, which
is the exact failure `CENTRING-VERDICT.md` set out to cure. The design as written is right for one
destination and wrong for the general portrait case, and it does not currently ask which it is.
`SPLASH_CHANNEL` already knows: `social-vertical` should get the 979 px stage, any other portrait
target should get the frame minus its margins. That branch is not built here.

**2 — The wireframe's ladder order was wrong where it mattered most, and rendering is what told me.**
I put reclassification above the standfirst rungs on the argument that a sentence is recoverable
outside the frame and shape detail is not. That argument is fine and the conclusion was backwards: a
histogram's shape *is* its claim, so its bins are the last thing to touch, not a middle thing. §4.1
of the wireframe records the change rather than hiding it. **I would not trust any of this ladder's
order that has not been rendered.**

**3 — The outcome is decided by about a dozen pixels, and that is a real weakness.** The histogram
refused at 13 px short of its plot floor; the line lost its entire standfirst to a 4 px shortfall.
The binding number is `plotWidth / maxAspect`, and `maxAspect` was derived by rounding a measured
extreme **outward to a tenth** — so the constraint is only known to about ±0.05, which on these
plots is ±25 px. **The ladder enforces to the pixel a bound that is known to twenty-five.** I did not
add a tolerance, because a fudge factor is exactly the kind of value nobody chose; but a tolerance
derived from the range's own rounding step would be defensible and would have kept both standfirsts.
That is a decision for the spec, not for a probe.

**4 — Yes, portrait should sometimes refuse — and the refusal I hit was not the interesting one.**
The brief records refusal as the leading hypothesis and the machinery is built and fires: the
histogram refused, loud, with the two numbers in the message. But it refused a beat that a human
would have shipped, over 13 px. The refusals that will matter are the ones the brief predicts —
twelve series, a scatter with two hundred labelled points — and **this probe did not test one.** The
mechanism exists; the threshold that makes it trustworthy does not.

**5 — On which types want a different FORM rather than a different size.** The earlier probe's
transposed ranking (`r-c-bars-transposed`) beat every other arm, and Horak et al. §2.4.2 gives the
rule and its limits independently. Carried forward unchanged, with the literature's boundary
attached:

- **Transpose in portrait** — bar, column, lollipop, dumbbell, diverging bar, and anything else whose
  category axis is a band scale. "converting a vertical bar chart into a horizontal one" (Horak
  §2.4.2); "Horizontal bar charts are a good option when your items have long names" (NN/g). The
  labels stay horizontal, which the same section insists on: "text should remain un-rotated to
  ensure its legibility".
- **Do not transpose** — histogram (a continuous x on a band scale would lie), line ("Line charts
  also resist rotation, due to the convention that the horizontal axis represents time proceeding
  from left to right"), scatter ("rotating a scatterplot would violate conventions of reading
  direction"), map ("rotating a map reduces viewers' ability to recognize familiar geographic
  features").
- **A third answer the literature offers and this probe did not test**: change the encoding rather
  than the shape — a paired bar to a slope chart, a population pyramid to overlaid curves (Horak
  §2.4.9, citing Camoes and Bremer). That is a bigger claim than transposition and it should be
  rendered before it is believed.

## What this does not close

- **Nothing was looked at on a phone.** Every figure here is arithmetic against published
  breakpoints and safe zones. One screenshot of `m-h-mobile-first.png` in a real story composer would
  test more than the rest of this file.
- **"How many series / categories / ticks can a phone frame carry" is still unanswered**, and §1.3 of
  the wireframe says why: nobody publishes a number. The budget stands in for it. Whether a
  budget-derived count matches what a designer would choose is untested.
- **The line's aspect range is known to be wrong** (its floor was learned from a square render that
  was already stretched) and is used anyway, so the line arm inherits a defect it did not create.
- **Only two types**, and neither takes R0. The transposition claim rests on the earlier probe plus
  the literature, not on anything rendered here. Nothing at all is known about maps, which have no
  plot rectangle for the budget to solve.
- **The title is a budget item the ladder cannot touch.** A 62-character headline takes three lines
  and a quarter of the stage, and the tool does not write text. Today that cost simply lands on the
  chart. Whether a portrait beat should tell the journalist "this headline costs you the standfirst"
  is an open question and probably the highest-value one left.
- **Nothing here is wired into any production component.** Two probe components, one runner, three
  renders, the wireframe and this file. `skills/` and every beat under `proof/` are untouched.
